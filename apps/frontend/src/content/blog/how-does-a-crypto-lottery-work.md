A crypto lottery works like any lottery at its core: you buy a ticket, a draw happens on a schedule, and a prize gets paid out to whoever matches the winning numbers. What changes is how the parts underneath work.

## The basic mechanics

Most crypto lotteries follow the same shape:

1. **You buy a ticket**, priced in a stablecoin like USDC instead of local currency. That means the price doesn't move around with token volatility, it's just $1, the same as it was yesterday.
2. **A draw happens on a fixed schedule.** Some run weekly like a traditional lottery, others run daily.
3. **Prizes are split into tiers** based on how many numbers you match, plus sometimes a bonus number.
4. **Payout happens on-chain**, so winnings land directly rather than going through a claims office.

## What's actually different from a state lottery

The mechanics look similar on paper, but three things change in practice:

- **You don't need to be near a retailer.** A crypto lottery is a smart contract, not a physical counter, so anyone with a wallet (or an app that manages one for you) can buy in from anywhere.
- **The prize pool and draw results are checkable.** Instead of trusting a press release, the contract holding the funds and the draw outcome are both on a public blockchain.
- **Draws can run more often.** Because there's no physical infrastructure to reset between draws, some crypto lotteries run daily instead of once or twice a week.

## Before you play

Crypto lotteries are still lotteries: the odds favor the house over any individual ticket, and rules on age and eligible countries apply just like a regular platform's terms of service. Treat it as entertainment you're paying for, not an investment, and check what's allowed where you live before you buy in.
