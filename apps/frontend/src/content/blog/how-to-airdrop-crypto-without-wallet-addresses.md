Every airdrop guide starts the same way: build a spreadsheet of wallet addresses. That's also where most airdrops stall. You don't have a spreadsheet of wallet addresses. You have a Discord member list, a GitHub contributor list, a newsletter of emails, a Telegram group, or a pile of X accounts that showed up for your last giveaway. None of those are wallet addresses, and turning them into one means DMing every single person, waiting, and losing a chunk of the list to people who never reply.

The fix isn't a better spreadsheet. It's paying the list you already have.

## The list you already have

You don't need to go collect anything. Wherever your community actually lives, you already have an identity list, just not a wallet list:

1. A waitlist or newsletter gives you emails.
2. An open-source repo gives you a GitHub contributor list.
3. A Discord server gives you a member list.
4. A Telegram group gives you usernames.
5. An X account gives you the people who followed, replied, or quote-tweeted your last post.

Every one of those identities can already be paid directly. Nobody on that list needs to have a wallet, or even know what one is, before you send anything.

## Multi-send: pay the list, not the wallets

Loofta's multi-send takes exactly that list and turns it into real USDC in people's hands. You pay up to 10 recipients in one batch, and each recipient is identified by email, username, X handle, Discord handle, GitHub handle, or Telegram handle, not an address. Paste a spreadsheet or CSV in `recipient,amount,message` format, review the total, and sign once from your own wallet. If a handle's platform isn't obvious from the identifier itself, prefix it, like `TELEGRAM:@alice,10,thanks for testing`, and multi-send routes it correctly.

That's the whole list-building problem solved by not having the problem: the list you export from Discord, GitHub, or your CRM is the input, with no address-collection step in between.

## What the recipient actually sees

The person on the other end doesn't need a wallet ready to go. They get notified their payout is waiting, click through to claim it, and sign in with the same email or handle you sent it to. If they don't already have a wallet, Loofta creates one for them right there, on the spot. The money is already waiting when they show up, so the wallet is a two-second afterthought instead of a prerequisite.

## Reward first, wallet second

This is the actual unlock: most people won't set up a crypto wallet speculatively for a maybe-someday airdrop. They will absolutely set one up in the ten seconds it takes to claim money that's already sitting there with their name on it. Flipping the order, reward first, wallet second, is why paying a list of humans converts so much better than asking a list of humans to first go get an address.
