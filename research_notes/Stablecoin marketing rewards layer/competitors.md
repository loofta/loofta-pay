# Competitors and substitutes for a stablecoin marketing rewards layer

Product under analysis: a campaign layer where a brand funds a prize in USDC/token/tickets into escrow; users complete X/Telegram/Discord tasks; winners are drawn on-chain or paid via a weekly raid leaderboard; payout is automatic. List price ~$15 + 5% of prize. Recurring raid packages ~$39/week, $99/4 weeks, $249/3 months.

Research date: 12 October 2026. Prices move; figures below are from vendor pages or dated secondary sources as cited.

## Direct crypto quest, raffle, and raid competitors

### Takeaway

The crypto “complete tasks, earn points or a raffle ticket” market is crowded and now includes a close substitute for Loofta’s USDC raffle: Zealy shipped custodial USDC lock-and-auto-payout in September 2025 at $0–$149+/month. Galxe remains the scale leader but prices like enterprise SaaS ($999/month billed annually). Layer3 automates on-chain token claims but publishes no dollar price and requires a wallet. Raid tools exist as a gray-market engagement layer ($49–$500/month) without prize escrow. No live vendor found that sells the full stack as a **campaign take-rate** ($15 + 5%) with **on-chain escrow**.

### Inferences

- Direct competition for “X/Discord tasks → raffle or leaderboard → USDC” is **Zealy**, not Galxe. Galxe is the distribution brand marketers already know, but it sells $12k–$20k/year seats plus participant claim fees. Loofta’s $15 + 5% is a different buying motion (campaign, not subscription).
- Zealy’s USDC lock is **custodial platform escrow**, not on-chain escrow. That is the cleanest technical distinction left. For a brand’s own token, Zealy still makes the organizer send payouts by hand — Layer3 and (if built) Loofta beat Zealy there.
- Raid packages at $39/week ($156/month) undercut documented raid-bot Pro ($500/month) and Galxe Essentials ($999), and sit in Gleam/KickoffLabs price territory. The risk is that “raid” in crypto Twitter usually means wash engagement, which is the exact failure mode that made quest products toxic.
- Alphabot/PREMINT own NFT allowlist raffles. They are substitutes only when the prize is a mint spot, not cash. Highlight is adjacent creator infra, not a competitor.
- Superboard is a live but quieter Galxe-class on-chain quest board. Treat it as a long-tail rival, not a pricing benchmark (no public price).

### Gaps

- RaidRush: no reliable homepage, pricing, or liveness confirmation found in this pass.
- X Spaces giveaway bots: no named, priced product found that is more than Discord/Telegram bots plus manual winner posts.
- Layer3 L3 stake amount, Domino per-active-member rate, TaskOn/QuestN per-claim fees: unpublished.
- Coinvise current product surface and pricing: not confirmed from a live product page.
- Gleam “crypto” mode: no evidence Gleam pays USDC; it is a Web2 contest tool that crypto projects sometimes use.

## Web2 contest and giveaway substitutes

### Takeaway

Web2 contest software is a mature, price-compressed category ($0–$99/month for SMBs) that already does verified social actions + random draw. It does **not** escrow crypto or auto-pay winners. Rafflecopter, a 15-year incumbent, shut down on 1 October 2025 without a public post-mortem. Loofta’s $15 + 5% can beat Gleam on total cost **if the prize is USDC and payout labor is included**; it loses if the brand only needs emails and Instagram follows.

### Cited Findings

- Gleam Competitions Pro is listed at $29/month on the Gleam apps pricing page. Gleam states a Free plan can run a “decent, minimal campaign.” Plans can be bought monthly or yearly; prices are USD. — [Gleam pricing](https://gleam.io/pricing); [How much does Gleam cost?](https://gleam.io/faq/accounts-and-billing/billing/how-much-does-gleam-cost)
- Gleam Package (all four apps) published: Pro $119/mo monthly or $79/mo annual; Business $479/mo or $299/mo; Premium $1,199/mo or $749/mo. Winner caps: Hobby 25 yearly; Pro 50 monthly / 100 yearly; Business 250 / 500; Premium 500 / 1,000. Live Draw is unlimited on package tiers checked. — [Gleam package pricing](https://gleam.io/pricing/package); [How many prizes can I give away?](https://gleam.io/faq/setup/how-many-prizes-can-i-give-away)
- Viral Loops annual: Start-up $35/mo (1,000 participants), Plus $99/mo (5,000), Growing $159/mo (10,000, includes automated rewarding, Stripe rewarding, Tremendous rewarding), Premium $279/mo. Monthly list prices are higher ($49 / $139 / $229 / $399). 14-day trial of Growing is capped at 10 participants. — [Viral Loops pricing](https://viral-loops.com/pricing)
- KickoffLabs annual: Hobby $13/mo (500 leads/month), Premium $48/mo (2,500), Business $99/mo (10,000), Enterprise $202/mo (25,000). Month-to-month $19 / $69 / $145 / $289. Unlimited campaigns; fraud prevention on Hobby. Checked against Viral Loops on 3 Sep 2026. — [KickoffLabs pricing](https://kickofflabs.com/pricing/); [KickoffLabs vs Viral Loops](https://kickofflabs.com/why-kickofflabs/compared-to-viral-loops/)
- ShortStack pricing page (checked by a 20 Sep 2026 roundup against shortstack.com): Free $0 (500 views / 200 entries); Launch $24/mo annual ($290/yr); Build $49; Scale $99; Max $166. Billing FAQ still lists older month/year pairs (Launch $29/mo or $288/year, etc.) — treat the pricing page as current and the FAQ as possibly stale. — [ShortStack pricing](https://www.shortstack.com/pricing/); [ShortStack billing FAQs](https://www.shortstack.com/help/billing-faqs/); [iTechGuides ShortStack pricing, 20 Sep 2026](https://www.itechguides.com/products/shortstack/pricing/)

### Inferences

- Web2 contest SaaS already owns “follow, tweet, join Discord, refer a friend, random draw” at prices **below** Loofta’s recurring raid packages and far below Galxe. The missing piece they all share is prize settlement. Viral Loops Growing is the exception: it wires Stripe and Tremendous for automated fiat/gift-card rewarding — a direct substitute for Loofta’s payout leg, still without crypto escrow.
- Rafflecopter’s quiet death is a demand signal (Gleam, ShortStack hunting those users) **and** a warning: giveaway software is a low-moat, high-churn category. Fifteen years of brand usage did not prevent shutdown.
- For a CPG or Web2 marketer, Gleam + Tremendous is the incumbent stack: $29/month software + 0–6% payout fees, fiat, compliance-known. Loofta has to beat that on speed-to-pay and global reach (wallets vs PayPal), not on contest UX.

### Gaps

- Woobox official 2026 price card not fetched from woobox.com.
- KickoffLabs “SMS contests” add-on price beyond the $50 Enterprise inclusion not itemized.
- Whether any Web2 contest vendor has shipped USDC or Bitcoin payouts: no evidence found; Giftbit/Tango Bitcoin e-gift cards are the nearest adjacent (see payout section).

## Payout and rewards infrastructure substitutes

### Takeaway

Mass-payout rails are a saturated, well-priced category. Tremendous, Tango, and Giftbit are **free platforms** with 0% on gift cards and 4–6% on cash. PayPal Payouts is 2% capped at $1 (web) or $0.25 flat (API, USD). Stripe Connect is $2/active account/month + 0.25% + $0.25 per payout when the platform sets pricing. None of these verify X/Telegram tasks or escrow a prize on-chain. Tremendous does **not** list USDC/crypto. Giftbit and Tango offer Bitcoin e-gift cards, which is “crypto-shaped cash,” not wallet USDC.

### Cited Findings

- Tremendous: $0 platform/subscription/API. Gift cards, prepaid cards, donations at face value. Venmo/PayPal/Cash App/bank: 4% if sender pays (min $0.25 USD for most USD products) or 6% if passed to recipient (USD min $1, max $25). Credit-card funding 3%; US/EU/UK bank funding $0. Catalog 2,300–2,500+ options, 200+ countries. No USDC in documented monetary options. — [Tremendous pricing](https://www.tremendous.com/pricing/); [Monetary option fees](https://help.tremendous.com/en/articles/monetary-option-fees)
- Giftbit: $0 subscription. Most digital rewards at face value; credit-card funding 2.9%. Promotional (expiring) rewards return 25% of unclaimed value to sender by default. Bitcoin e-gift cards listed. No bank/Venmo/PayPal. — [Giftbit pricing](https://www.giftbit.com/pricing); [Giftbit vs Tremendous vs Tango](https://www.giftbit.com/tremendous-tango-alternatives)
- Tango: no minimums, no sign-up fees; pay face value. Giftbit’s comparison (vendor-authored) lists Tango credit-card funding at 3.5%, optional $450 Reward Link customization, $0.25 per expiring reward, 75% breakage share, Bitcoin e-gift cards, plus ACH/PayPal via Payouts Link. Tango homepage confirms portal is “self-serve, free to use.” — [Tango homepage](https://www.tangocard.com/); [Giftbit comparison](https://www.giftbit.com/tremendous-tango-alternatives)
- Bitrefill is a **consumer** crypto-to-gift-card/card product (no monthly/issuance fee on the card; variable crypto-to-EUR spread; 2.49% ATM; €10/month inactivity after 12 months), not a brand mass-payout API for campaigns. — [Bitrefill card pricing](https://www.bitrefill.com/card/pricing)
- PayPal Payouts (ex–Mass Pay): web 2% of each payment, USD cap $1.00; Payouts API USD $0.25 flat. International 2% with currency caps. Max $60,000 to existing PayPal/Venmo accounts ($20,000 new). Funded from PayPal balance. — [PayPal US fees](https://securepayments.paypal.com/us/business/paypal-business-fees); [PayPal Payouts](https://www.paypal.com/us/business/make-payments/mass-payments)
- Stripe Connect: if Stripe bills connected accounts, no extra platform fee. If the platform sets pricing: $2 per monthly active account + 0.25% + $0.25 per payout to bank/debit. — [Stripe Connect pricing](https://stripe.com/connect/pricing)
- Tipalti: vendor blog lists Starter AP at $99/month; a third-party comparison says Mass Payments from $249/month, with anecdotal Premium implementation $4k–$5k and $500–$600/month. — [Tipalti marketplace guide](https://tipalti.com/blog/online-marketplace-payments/); [Stripe Connect vs Tipalti](https://usedots.com/compare/stripe-connect-vs-tipalti/)
- Rybbon: not independently priced in this pass; historically a research-incentive payout tool overlapping Tremendous/Tango. Leave as an unpriced Tremendous peer.

### Inferences

- Loofta’s 5% of prize is **in the same band** as Tremendous cash (4–6%) and worse than PayPal API ($0.25) or Stripe payout (0.25%+$0.25) for domestic fiat. The 5% is only justified if Loofta also does verification + escrow + draw. Against Tremendous gift cards at 0%, 5% looks expensive unless the recipient wants USDC in a wallet.
- Viral Loops Growing already composes “contest software + Tremendous.” That is the Web2 version of Loofta’s stack, minus crypto.
- A Web2 brand that is not trying to get users on-chain will pick Tremendous. Loofta’s payout-infra buyers are crypto-native campaigns and global recipients who do not have PayPal.

### Gaps

- Rybbon current pricing and owner (Tango acquired Rybbon historically; current brand status not re-verified).
- Tremendous crypto: search of pricing and monetary-option docs found no USDC/USDT. Absence from docs is not a legal guarantee they will never add it.
- Relish in the user prompt sits with payout infra in some taxonomies; live Relish (tryrelish.com) is a **local creator/UGC campaign** product, not Tremendous-class rewards. See influencer section.

## Influencer and UGC platform substitutes

### Takeaway

Influencer/UGC platforms sell **paid creators**, not prize-funded user tasks. Entry contracts run ~$400/month (Insense) to $12k–$40k/year (Aspire, GRIN). Billo is pay-per-video from $99. TikTok Creative Challenge was folded into TikTok One (March 2025), which is an influencer marketplace with 10k-follower eligibility. These are substitutes only when the brand’s real goal is content, not community campaigns. They do not escrow a user prize pool.

### Cited Findings

- Aspire: no public pricing page (aspire.io/pricing 404 per CompareUGC). Third-party 2026 roundups cite ~$1,000–$2,000/month, often annual; Vendr range $11,139–$36,599/year, median $15,588. Creator pay extra. Capterra has listed $2,499/user/month — treat as inconsistent with Vendr. — [CompareUGC Aspire](https://compareugc.com/platforms/aspire); [AgencyUGC Aspire pricing 2026](https://agencyugc.com/blog/aspire-pricing-for-agencies)
- GRIN: custom; one 2025 alternative guide cites $25k–$40k/year. CompareUGC lists GRIN starting price as “$0 / Subscription” which conflicts — likely a catalog error. Prefer the $25k–$40k range as a reported enterprise floor, not a verified rate card. — [Insense alternatives, GetSaral](https://www.getsaral.com/academy/insense-alternatives)
- Insense (checked 9 Oct 2026 per Billo): $400/month billed annually, $500/month quarterly, or $650 one-month trial, plus creator payments and a marketplace fee of 20% (Trial) / 10% (Brand) / 7% (Agency). — [Billo vs Insense](https://billo.app/blog/billo-vs-insense/)
- Billo: from $99 per video in packs; no monthly fee in months with no orders. Older GetSaral page listed packs $500/5 videos to $2,500/30 — inconsistent with Billo’s own Oct 2026 blog; prefer Billo. — [Billo vs Insense](https://billo.app/blog/billo-vs-insense/)
- TikTok Creator Marketplace / Creative Challenge rebranded; after 10 March 2025 brand campaigns route through TikTok One. Eligibility cited: 10k+ followers, 100k+ likes in 28 days, 3+ posts in 30 days with 1k+ views, age 18+. Sponsored posts in the 10k–100k follower band commonly $100–$500 (secondary). — [30DayPivot UGC platforms](https://30daypivot.com/ugc_creator_spoke_platforms)
- Relish (tryrelish.com): local creator shopper-marketing / UGC Hub for restaurants and CPG; 20–200+ local creators. No public brand-side plan price as of a 2 Sep 2026 review. Public creator campaigns pay roughly $50–$100 cash plus comps (e.g. $60 minus PayPal fee, $70, up to $150 dining credit, $100 for a Claim referral campaign). — [Relish products](https://tryrelish.com/products); [Feastalytics Relish vs Remy](https://feastalytics.com/blog/remy-vs-relish); [Relish campaigns](https://tryrelish.com/campaigns)

### Inferences

- Loofta is cheaper than every named influencer SaaS by an order of magnitude. That is not a win unless the brand will accept **user** UGC/raids instead of **creator** UGC. Most CPG budgets are built for the latter.
- Insense’s 7–20% on creator pay is the closest fee analogue to Loofta’s 5% of prize — but the principal is creator invoices, not a prize pool.
- Relish shows Web2 already pays $15–$100 per completed local action with receipt verification. Loofta’s “complete a social task, enter a raffle” is a lower-trust, lower-quality version of the same idea.

### Gaps

- #paid: no reliable 2026 public price card found.
- Grin official rate card not on a public pricing page.
- TikTok One brand-side fees (beyond creator post rates) not found.

## Loyalty and cashback substitutes

### Takeaway

Shopify loyalty (Smile, LoyaltyLion, Stamped) is a different job: points on **purchases**, not campaign tasks. Prices start at $0–$23/month and jump to $199–$999 as orders scale. They do not escrow USDC prizes or verify tweets. They matter as the default “rewards” budget line a DTC brand already pays, which crowds Loofta out of recurring retain spend and leaves Loofta in **acquisition/campaign** spend.

### Cited Findings

- Smile.io: Free (200 orders/month); paid from $15/month; popular Growth $199/month; Plus $999/month (7,500 orders, annual billing per one TCO guide). Shopify-centric. — [Smile.io pricing](https://smile.io/pricing); [Loyalty platform pricing 2026](https://exchangesolutions.com/resources/loyalty-platform-pricing-2026/)
- LoyaltyLion: Exchange Solutions 2026 guide lists Classic from $199/month for 500 orders; Smile’s own comparison says Classic ~$399/month and Advanced ~$699/month for VIP tiers. **Conflict:** $199 vs ~$399 for Classic. Both are 2026 sources; Smile is a competitor. Prefer “starts $199–$399/month, VIP much higher” until LoyaltyLion’s live page is checked. — [Exchange Solutions](https://exchangesolutions.com/resources/loyalty-platform-pricing-2026/); [Smile vs LoyaltyLion](https://smile.io/learn/smile-vs-loyaltylion)
- Stamped Loyalty Shopify app: Basic $23/month (200 orders), Premium $59, Business $149; platform plans $199–$798/month. Lite plan discontinued June 2025. — [Smile vs Stamped, LoyalDaddy](https://loyaldaddy.com/smile-io-vs-stamped)
- Thanx: no public price; positioned as white-label. — [TechYorker Thanx vs LoyaltyLion vs Smile](https://techyorker.com/compare/thanx-vs-loyaltylion-vs-smile-io/)
- Reward funding, not software, dominates loyalty TCO: one 2026 model for a large retailer put reward funding at ~73% of program spend. — [Exchange Solutions](https://exchangesolutions.com/resources/loyalty-platform-pricing-2026/)

### Inferences

- A Shopify brand with Smile at $15–$199/month already “has rewards.” Selling them a second rewards layer only works for **launches, raids, and giveaways**, not for replacing loyalty.
- Cashback apps (Claim, via Relish campaigns) pay users for **real purchases**, which is the quality bar Loofta raids will be compared against.

### Gaps

- Stamp vs Stamped: user listed “Stamp”; live Shopify product found is Stamped. Confirm whether “Stamp” meant Stamped or another product.
- Thanx, LoyaltyLion, and cashback apps (Rakuten, Fetch, Claim) official 2026 cards not all fetched.

## Dead, pivoted, and failed products — and why

### Takeaway

Quest marketplaces die from **farmer/sybil economics and missing moat**, not from lack of demand. Intract is the cleanest corpse: 1M weekly users to <50k, then a 12 Jan 2026 shutdown, after brands learned they could copy the front end. RabbitHole pivoted to Boost (2024) rather than die in place. Crew3 survived by becoming Zealy and adding USDC. Rafflecopter (Web2) died after 15 years with no stated reason. The landmine for Loofta is the same as Intract’s: if the product is “a quest board with a leaderboard,” a large brand will DIY; if payouts are easy to farm, the brand churns.

### Cited Findings

- Intract Quest closed 12 January 2026 (official sunset page). Self-reported 12M+ questers, 5,000+ partnerships, $10M+ rewards. Domain intract.io reused by a payments company; old docs still describe a live product, which is why 2025–2026 listicles still recommend it. — [quest.intract.io](https://quest.intract.io/); [Zealy alternatives survey](https://zealy.io/blog/best-galxe-alternatives)
- Intract post-mortem (company LinkedIn, 2026): built as “Amazon for crypto brands”; flywheel reversed when a few large brands left because “any of them could build their own front end & get users cheaper”; weekly users fell from 1,000,000 to below 50,000. — [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/intract-io_activity-7490649623930454016-pQOd)
- RabbitHole announced Boost Inbox as successor (live Feb 2024, RabbitHole merge by early April 2024). Quest Protocol became Boost. Framed as evolution, not a shutdown. GitHub quest-protocol still documents escrow-like Protocol Rewards and claim/cancel flows. — [RabbitHole Mirror post](https://rabbithole.mirror.xyz/im48l7lxJs893QzdiykwHYe5Og6ZEemUjhBWJMsmdD4?collectors=true); [quest-protocol README](https://github.com/rabbitholegg/quest-protocol/)
- Crew3 → Zealy (April 2023), $3.5M raised; rebrand explicitly to go beyond web3. — [Zealy changelog](https://zealy.io/changelog/2023-04-06-zealy); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/04/03/zealy-is-an-achievement-system-for-web3-communities/)
- Rafflecopter closed 1 Oct 2025; no official cause. — [rafflecopter.com](https://www.rafflecopter.com/)
- PREMINT Collector Pass expired end of 2024; product still runs allowlists. This is NFT-market winter shrinkage, not a full death. — [premint.xyz](https://www.premint.xyz/); [PREMINT GitBook](https://premint.gitbook.io/premint/guides/expired-offering-spots-to-premint-collector-pass-holders)
- QuestN showed stale 2025 footer, zeroed marketing counters, and a July 2024 iOS build as of 12 Aug 2026 — “possibly zombie,” not confirmed dead. — [Zealy alternatives survey](https://zealy.io/blog/best-galxe-alternatives)
- Airdrop-farmer interviews (Odaily/WuBlockchain, covering 2023–2025): Galxe campaigns remain attractive because costs (RPC, gas, farming) are calculable; 2024 profits lagged 2023; sybil clustering is stricter; farmers use fingerprint browsers and mix automation with manual actions; “bulk farming is expensive, and once flagged as a Sybil, the losses can be devastating.” — [WuBlockchain / Odaily](https://wublockchain.medium.com/odaily-interviews-airdrop-farmers-how-to-obtain-crypto-airdrops-in-2025-6e111a8b2a93)
- Galxe itself productized the problem: 2025 Earndrop + Business+ “bot vs real user” exports; Advanced Sybil Prevention gated to Growth/Enterprise. — [Galxe 2025 review](https://www.galxe.com/blog/galxe-2025-year-in-review); [Quest Performance Data](https://help.galxe.com/en/articles/11586905-business-quest-performance-data-leaderboard-insights)
- ChainScore (vendor blog, treat as advocacy not audit) claims sybil-farmed addresses show >90% post-airdrop churn; gamified onboarding 25–40% 90-day retention; “$500M+ annual drain” on treasuries — **unsourced**. Same site also argues poorly designed quests subsidize Sybil attacks. — [ChainScore gamification](https://chainscorelabs.com/blog/airdrop-strategies-and-community-building/gamified-engagement-and-quests/why-your-airdrop-strategy-is-failing-without-gamification); [ChainScore quest network effects](https://chainscorelabs.com/blog/airdrop-strategies-and-community-building/gamified-engagement-and-quests/the-future-of-user-acquisition-quest-driven-network-effects)

### Inferences

- Failure mode 1 — **sybil/token-farming**: cheap tasks (follow, join, like) are an API for farmers. If Loofta’s raid leaderboard scores follows/replies without impression or uniqueness checks, it becomes a paid wash-trading desk. Zealy’s min-likes/impressions threshold is the current best published counter.
- Failure mode 2 — **no warehouse moat (Intract)**: marketplace of brands × questers is not defensible. Escrow + automatic payout + compliance is more like Amazon’s logistics — closer to a moat, still copyable.
- Failure mode 3 — **NFT raffle winter**: PREMINT/Alphabot demand tracks mint volume. USDC prizes are a hedge; they also attract farmers who only want cash.
- Failure mode 4 — **regulation**: no shutdown in this set was clearly attributed to securities or sweepstakes law. That does **not** mean Loofta is clear: Web2 contest tools (Gleam) lean on official rules, winner caps, and geo blocking because US sweepstakes law is real. Crypto raffles of USDC for social tasks can look like an illegal lottery (prize + chance + consideration). This pass did not find a quest-platform prosecution; it is still a legal landmine, not a researched conclusion.
- RabbitHole’s pivot implies on-chain “do a swap, get tokens” needed a new wrapper (Boost) after the 2021–22 quest hype. Social-task USDC raffles are earlier in that hype cycle for Web2 brands, later (fatigued) for crypto natives.

- No detailed independent post-mortem for Rafflecopter.
- No confirmed shutdown of Superboard, QuestN, or Coinvise — only weakness signals.
- No 2024–2026 enforcement action found in this pass that killed a quest/giveaway product.

## Honest crowdedness rating and the wedge that is not already owned

### Takeaway

**Category crowdedness: saturated** for incentivized tasks/giveaways; **crowded** for crypto quest SaaS; **sparse** for the exact bundle “on-chain prize escrow + verified social/raid quality + automatic USDC payout + Gleam-like campaign pricing.” The wedge that is not already owned is **not** “quests for USDC” — Zealy shipped that. The unowned wedge is **trust-minimized escrow + campaign take-rate + quality-weighted raids**, sold as a Gleam-simple campaign instead of a $149–$999/month growth OS.

### Cited Findings

- Live Galxe alternatives Zealy opened on 12 Aug 2026: Zealy, Guild.xyz, TaskOn, QuestN, Layer3, Domino, plus Galxe itself. Intract dead. Published entry prices $29 (Guild) to $1,200 (QuestN) vs Galxe $999. — [Zealy alternatives survey](https://zealy.io/blog/best-galxe-alternatives)
- Web2 contest substitutes live at $13–$99/month (KickoffLabs, Gleam Pro, ShortStack, Viral Loops) with Rafflecopter gone. — [KickoffLabs](https://kickofflabs.com/pricing/); [Gleam](https://gleam.io/pricing); [ShortStack](https://www.shortstack.com/pricing/); [Viral Loops](https://viral-loops.com/pricing); [Rafflecopter](https://www.rafflecopter.com/)
- Payout substitutes at $0 platform + 0–6% (Tremendous, Giftbit, Tango) or cents-per-payout (PayPal, Stripe). — [Tremendous](https://www.tremendous.com/pricing/); [PayPal](https://www.paypal.com/us/business/make-payments/mass-payments); [Stripe Connect](https://stripe.com/connect/pricing)
- Zealy USDC auto-distribution launched 17 Sep 2025 with lock-at-creation guarantees. — [Zealy USDC Rewards](https://zealy.io/changelog/2025-09-17-usdc-rewards)
- Loofta list prices in the brief: ~$15 + 5% of prize; raid packs ~$39/week, $99/4 weeks, $249/3 months. Worked example: $1,000 prize → $15 + $50 = $65 all-in vs Zealy Standard $149/month (and X tasks require paid) vs Galxe $999/month vs Gleam $29/month **plus manual shipping of the prize**.

### Inferences

Crowdedness scores (researcher judgment, not a vendor metric):

| Layer | Rating | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Crypto quest boards | **Saturated** | Galxe, Zealy, Layer3, TaskOn, QuestN, Superboard, Domino, plus zombies and listicle ghosts |
| NFT raffle/allowlist | **Crowded / declining** | PREMINT, Alphabot; demand tracks mints |
| Raid / engagement tools | **Crowded, low-trust** | Telegram bots, paid raid pools; wash engagement |
| Web2 contests | **Saturated** | Gleam, Viral Loops, ShortStack, KickoffLabs, Woobox; Rafflecopter exit |
| Mass payout | **Saturated** | Tremendous/Tango/Giftbit/PayPal/Stripe/Tipalti |
| Influencer UGC | **Crowded** | Aspire, GRIN, Insense, Billo, TikTok One |
| Shopify loyalty | **Crowded** | Smile, LoyaltyLion, Stamped, Yotpo |
| On-chain escrow + social verify + auto USDC + campaign pricing | **Sparse** | Zealy has custodial USDC + SaaS pricing; Layer3 has on-chain pay + no social/SMB price; Gleam has price + no crypto pay |

**The wedge that is not owned:**

1. **Price architecture.** Sell a campaign (or a week of raids) the way Gleam sells a contest, not the way Galxe sells a space. A marketing manager with a $1,000 USDC giveaway will not approve $999/month. They might approve $65. Zealy at $149/month still wins always-on community teams; Loofta should not fight that ICP.
2. **On-chain escrow, not a platform IOU.** Zealy locks USDC in Zealy. If Zealy sunsets like Intract, the lock is a bankruptcy/admin risk. On-chain escrow with a public draw (Galxe’s zkRaffle direction, Layer3’s claim contract) is the trust story Web2 contest tools cannot tell and Zealy has not shipped.
3. **Pay for raid quality, not quest completion.** Follow/join/like is how Galxe got farmer-toxic. Zealy’s impression/like thresholds and Viral Loops/KickoffLabs fraud tools show the market already knows this. A weekly leaderboard that pays USDC for **verified reach** (impressions, unique comment quality) against wash raids is closer to Relish’s “receipt + post” than to Galxe’s “follow and claim.”
4. **Do not build a two-sided quest marketplace.** Intract’s post-mortem is explicit: brands will not stay if they can clone the front end. Own the **settlement** (escrow, draw, payout, receipts), and be embeddable in Gleam-like or Discord-native flows.

Honest warning: if Loofta ships “create a Galxe-like quest board with USDC prizes,” it is late, crowded, and competing with Zealy’s free tier. If it ships “Gleam-priced, on-chain-escrowed, auto-paid campaigns and scored raids,” the slot is still open — and it will be judged on sybil quality, not on feature count.
