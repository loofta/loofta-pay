Scope note: Research compiled 13 Oct 2026. Covers historical wedges (PayPal, Venmo, Cash App, Wise, Tremendous, Payoneer, Hyperwallet, Tipalti, Deel, Mercury, Rippling, Remitly) plus 2023–2026 stablecoin-infra outcomes (Bridge, Privy, Helio, Iron, BVNK, TipLink/Fin, Grey, Airtm, Toku, Sling, Rise, LatAm neobanks, Revolut Revtag, Pix/UPI, Solana Pay, Colosseum payments alums). This pass also adds YouTube/PayPal PYUSD creator payouts, Gusto–Zero Hash contractor USDC, Cash App USDC-on-Solana, Sacra remittance/contractor-wallet framing, Dragonfly/Paradigm 2026 payments bets, Zebec as a going concern (not a failure), and Colosseum $250k / 0.7% accelerator terms. Self-reported numbers from press releases are labeled **(self-reported)**. Aggregators are labeled. Cases I could not source are in Gaps, not filled from memory.

Loofta context, for the writer: Solana. Pay any email or @username before the recipient has a wallet. Recipient claims USDC or 150+ tokens, or cashes out to Venmo, Cash App, Revolut, Zelle, or a Nigerian bank. Also: MagicBlock private transfers, escrowed giveaway/bounty rail, payment-links API + SDK, x402 agent path. One founder, about $800/month revenue, Colosseum alum.

## What was each company's initial wedge, distribution channel, and the moment it became a platform?

### Takeaway
Every compounder started with one high-frequency, painful flow and a distribution channel that recruited the other side of the transaction. It became a platform when a second party began building on it — merchants accepting Venmo, marketplaces integrating Payoneer, banks embedding Wise Platform, businesses misusing GiftRocket for bulk payouts, HR expanding from payroll into IT and spend, or YouTube/Gusto plugging an existing payout API. Consumer wedges only became valuable once someone else paid to plug in. Solana Pay itself never made that jump: it stayed a permissionless protocol, while the valuable Solana payments companies were the ones that owned merchants, cash-out, or a payout embed.

### Cited Findings

**PayPal (email as account + eBay density)**
- Confinity launched PayPal as “beaming money by email” in late 1999. The pitch: anyone with a credit card could pay anyone with an email address; the recipient signed up to claim. — [PayPal press release, 16 Nov 1999](https://www.paypalobjects.com/html/pr-111699.html)
- Viral loop: referral bonuses of $10 to sender and recipient. Growth compounded 7–10% daily. Users: 1,000 mid-Nov 1999 → 12,000 end-Dec → 100,000 by 3 Feb 2000 → 1 million by mid-April 2000. — [Peter Thiel, Startup Archive](https://www.startuparchive.org/p/peter-thiel-tells-the-founding-story-of-paypal)
- Primary 2 Feb 2000 release: 100,000 users since October debut; ~90% of signups in January; “more than 37,000 items are for sale on eBay with PayPal, more than twice the number of its nearest competitor”; “non-users become users when they claim money that has been emailed to them.” — [PayPal press release, 2 Feb 2000](https://www.paypalobjects.com/html/pr-020200.html)
- Thiel later: pure email virality “wasn’t as good because it didn’t come with a persistent engage use case,” whereas “virality tuned to eBay” created lock-in. Typical eBay ticket ~$40; checks took ~10 days; most sellers lacked card processing. — [Masters of Scale / Thiel](https://app.mastersofscale.com/content-item/hffsKpIDI4QnxjoMJjat); [Startup Archive](https://www.startuparchive.org/p/peter-thiel-tells-the-founding-story-of-paypal)
- eBay acquired PayPal in 2002 for $1.5 billion (all-stock). — [Wikipedia Timeline of PayPal, citing contemporaneous coverage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_PayPal)

**Venmo (P2P SMS, then merchant checkout via Braintree)**
- Launched 2009 as a text-message payment system. Braintree bought it Aug 2012 for $26.2M cash and stock. At purchase: ~$10M/month processed, ~30% monthly growth, 23-person team. — [NYT Bits](https://bits.blogs.nytimes.com/2012/08/16/payments-start-up-braintree-buys-venmo-for-26-2-million/); [Finovate](https://finovate.com/braintree-acquires-venmo-for-26-million/)
- Braintree bought it as a mobile-commerce wallet to plug into merchants (Uber, Airbnb, HotelTonight), not as a P2P business. CEO Bill Ready: preparing for “the coming wave of mobile commerce.” — [NYT Bits](https://bits.blogs.nytimes.com/2012/08/16/payments-start-up-braintree-buys-venmo-for-26-2-million/)
- PayPal bought Braintree (and Venmo) for $800M cash in Sep 2013. — [eco.com summary citing filings](https://eco.com/support/en/articles/17190465-who-owns-venmo-ownership-history-and-how-it-makes-money)
- Monetization came roughly a decade later. Core P2P stays free; revenue from instant transfers, card-funded sends, goods-and-services seller fees, Pay with Venmo, cards, crypto. — [eco.com](https://eco.com/support/en/articles/17190465-who-owns-venmo-ownership-history-and-how-it-makes-money)
- Venmo revenue ~$1.7B in 2025, up ~20%, excluding interest on balances (PayPal 2025 10-K as summarized). Pay with Venmo reached $1B TPV in Sep 2025 alone. Users of both Venmo Debit and Pay with Venmo generate >9x the ARPA of P2P-only users (PayPal Q2 2026 call). — [CoinLaw aggregator citing PayPal filings](https://coinlaw.io/venmo-statistics/)

**Cash App ($cashtag as public identity, then a banking ladder, then USDC)**
- Square Cash launched 13 Oct 2013 as P2P via email/phone + debit card. — [Wikipedia Cash App, citing contemporaneous coverage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash_App)
- 23 Mar 2015: Square opened Cash to businesses (1.5% “professional rate,” deposit to bank) and launched $Cashtags — public, claimable identifiers (`$ErinHills`) usable in-app or at cash.me, so businesses did not have to publish email/phone. At that point Square Cash was processing “over one billion dollars in peer-to-peer payments annualized” **(self-reported)**. — [Square press release](https://squareup.com/us/en/press/introducing-cashtags); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2015/03/23/businesstime-for-square-cash/)
- Bitcoin buy/sell rolled out to most US users 31 Jan 2018 (excluded NY, GA, HI, WY); $10k/week buy cap. Users could not yet send BTC to other Cash users. — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2018/01/31/jack-dorseys-square-launches-bitcoin-trading-for-all-cash-app-users-except-those-in-four-states.html)
- Block today: P2P “remains the foundation of our network and go-to-market strategy.” Users can send bitcoin to any phone number or $Cashtag, settling off-chain instantly, plus Lightning. The $cashtag namespace was reused as a crypto address book. — [Block Q2 2025 shareholder letter](https://cdn.yahoofinance.com/prod/sec-filings/0001512673/000119312525175456/d942331dex991.htm); [Block 10-K FY2024](https://app.edgar.tools/filing/1512673/0001628280-25-007376)
- Scale Sep/Oct 2025: 58M monthly transacting actives, 26M Cash App Card actives, 8.7M primary banking actives, 8.7M bitcoin accounts, 7.4M Borrow actives. — [Block Investor Day 2025](https://block.xyz/documents/block-investor-day-2025-go-to-market-strategy.pdf); [Block Q3 2025 letter](https://www.publicnow.com/view/9A66878FA994805582102C747C7ECA0D651804A1)
- Q3 2025: gross profit per transacting active $94 (up 25% YoY); inflows per active $1,366. 2024 inflows $283B. — [Block Q3 2025 letter](https://www.publicnow.com/view/9A66878FA994805582102C747C7ECA0D651804A1); [Block 10-K FY2024](https://app.edgar.tools/filing/1512673/0001628280-25-007376)
- Messari (aggregator, Feb 2026): Cash App is integrating USDC on Solana in early 2026; each user gets a dedicated Solana address; incoming USDC is credited as USD; outgoing can go to any Solana wallet. Messari cites “56 million monthly active users” for this rollout — slightly below Block’s 58M transacting-actives figure above; treat 56M as Messari’s figure, 58M as Block’s. — [Messari, State of Solana: Payments](https://messari.io/report/state-of-solana-payments)

**Tremendous (consumer gift site → bulk B2B payouts; profitable, no VC)**
- GiftRocket (YC 2011) was a consumer digital gift-card / cash e-card. By 2012 it was profitable on ~$2M sales, expected $5M run rate by December. It launched an API and bulk orders because “businesses and researchers” were using it to thank survey participants. — [TechCrunch 2012](https://techcrunch.com/2012/09/03/digital-gifting-startup-giftrocket-hits-profitability-announces-api-and-reduced-fees/)
- Founders spotted the B2B segment via SQL queries of companies sending incentives repeatedly. They bought out investors ($500K in) at 2x using cash flow from ~$400K/year revenue, ran the business in maintenance with ~1 person, relaunched as Tremendous in 2018. — [Indie Hackers podcast summary](https://www.signalcast.app/episode/the-indie-hackers-podcast/261-self-funding-pivoting-to-a-better-idea-and-growing-beyond-10m-with-kapil-kale-of-tremendous); [Just Entrepreneurs](https://justentrepreneurs.co.uk/meet-the-founders-interview-series/meet-the-co-founder-of-tremendousc)
- Today Tremendous reports $2B+ delivered, 90M+ payouts, 25K+ companies, 3K+ payout options, 200+ regions, 150+ employees, “profitable… with no outside investment” **(self-reported)**. Open self-serve signup (no sales call) drew inbound from companies like Google. Segments: researchers, marketers, HR, class-action administrators, nonprofits. — [Tremendous company page](https://www.tremendous.com/company-history/)

**Payoneer (marketplace receiving accounts → cross-border SMB platform)**
- Founded 2005 to pay global freelancers. Core product: a receiving account with local bank details that sellers hand to Amazon, Walmart, Upwork, Airbnb, or a client. Later added AP/AR, working capital, cards, checkout. — [PaymentCompanies.com](https://www.paymentcompanies.com/companies/payoneer/)
- Platform integrations (Amazon sellers in China, India, Eastern Europe; Fiverr; Upwork) each brought thousands of users. — [FreeMalta company story (secondary, lower quality)](https://freemalta.com/markets/company-stories/payoneer)
- FY2025 (primary): volume $87.5B (up 9% from $80.1B), revenue $1.053B (up 8% from $977.7M), take rate 120 bps (down 2 bps YoY), SMB take rate 118 bps (up 9 bps). Active ICPs 536k (down 4%). ARPU $488 (up 15%). Nearly 2 million active customers. — [Payoneer 10-K FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1845815/000110465926020487/payo-20251231x10k.htm); [Payoneer FY2025 results](https://investor.payoneer.com/news-releases/news-release-details/payoneer-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2025-financial)
- SPAC listing Jun 2021 at ~$3.3B EV. Nuvei agreed 15 Jun 2026 to buy it for $7.40/share cash, ~$2.75B equity value; close targeted mid-2027. Combined company expected ~$3B revenue and >$500B volume for >2.4M customers. Buyer explicitly cites Payoneer’s marketplace payouts, multi-currency accounts, licenses (incl. China online-payment licence and India RBI authorization in principle), and “agentic commerce, stablecoin payments.” — [Nuvei/Payoneer release](https://investor.payoneer.com/news-releases/news-release-details/nuvei-acquire-payoneer-275-billion-creating-leading-global)

**Hyperwallet (marketplace payouts infrastructure)**
- PayPal bought it for ~$400M cash (closed 15 Nov 2018). Stated rationale: payouts for “ecommerce platforms and marketplaces,” then >50% of global online retail sales (2017 figure), in 200+ markets. — [PayPal close release](https://newsroom.paypal-corp.com/2018-11-15-PayPal-Completes-Acquisition-of-Hyperwallet)
- PayPal’s 2025 10-K still counts Hyperwallet payees as “platform access partner” active accounts inside the 439M headline active-account number. — [PayPal 10-K FY2025](https://app.edgar.tools/filing/1633917/0001633917-26-000024)

**YouTube + PayPal Payouts (incumbent embed of stablecoin as a payout *option*)**
- Dec 2025: eligible US YouTube creators can opt in to receive earnings in PayPal’s PYUSD. PayPal head of crypto May Zabaneh confirmed it to Fortune; a Google spokesperson confirmed. US-only at launch. YouTube was already a PayPal Payouts customer for gig/creator disbursement. PayPal added “receive in PYUSD” capability early in Q3 2025; YouTube then turned the option on. YouTube itself does not custody crypto or talk to a chain. — [Fortune, 11 Dec 2025](https://fortune.com/2025/12/11/youtube-paypal-google-stablecoin-payouts-pyusd/)
- Cointelegraph: YouTube still sends USD into PayPal’s payout system; the change is at disbursement (PYUSD credit instead of bank/PayPal fiat). Creators can hold in PayPal, redeem to USD, or transfer to supported networks/wallets subject to PayPal fees. — [Cointelegraph Magazine, 17 Dec 2025](https://cointelegraph.com/magazine/how-youtube-s-stablecoin-payouts-could-change-creator-monetization)
- Messari restates this as “PYUSD on the Solana network.” Fortune does *not* say YouTube chose Solana; PYUSD happens to exist on Solana (Messari: $834.7M PYUSD on Solana as of 2 Feb 2025). Treat Solana as the asset’s venue, not YouTube’s integration choice. — [Messari, State of Solana: Payments](https://messari.io/report/state-of-solana-payments)

- Last equity valuation $8.3B (Series F, Dec 2021). Raised $150M Series F-II + Hercules/JPM in May 2023, then $200M growth *debt* from Hercules 24 Sep 2025. — [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/tipalti/financials); [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/tipalti-secures-200-million-in-growth-financing-to-drive-next-wave-of-ai-innovation-in-finance-302565177.html)
- Sep 2025 **(self-reported)**: >$200M ARR, customer base +30% YoY, >5,000 companies (Calm, Udemy, AG1, AppLovin), $75B annualized payment volume (+30% YoY), 200+ countries, 120 currencies. 2024: $182M revenue, $70B processed (Forbes/Wikipedia citing). — [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/tipalti-secures-200-million-in-growth-financing-to-drive-next-wave-of-ai-innovation-in-finance-302565177.html); [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/companies/tipalti/)

**Wise (GBP–EUR corridor → bank-embedded platform)**
- TransferWise went live 24 Jan 2011 as GBP↔EUR at mid-market plus a flat £1 fee, matching opposite flows instead of using correspondent banks. Founders’ own pain: Estonian salary/mortgage vs UK pounds. — [TechCrunch launch](https://techcrunch.com/2011/01/24/transferwise-wants-to-be-the-skype-of-currency-exchange/)
- First week: transfers between UK and France, Italy, Germany, Austria, Netherlands, Ireland, Belgium; average ~£1,000. By seed ($1.3M, Max Levchin + Index + IA Ventures, 2011): $13M exchanged, avg ~£1,500. Still UK–eurozone only. — [Startup Founder Stories citing founder posts](https://startupfounderstories.com/stories/kristo-kaarmann-wise-launch-week-transfers); [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/business/transferwise-undercuts-the-banks-with-crowdsourced-currency-exchange/)
- Corridor expansion was sequential: Europe first, then US/Australia (2015), US–Mexico (2016; then the world’s largest remittance corridor, ~$25B to Mexico in 2015). — [Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.com/transferwise-launches-mexico-us-transfers-biggest-money-corridor-in-the-world-2016-4)
- FY2025 (to Mar 2025): revenue £1.21B, cross-border revenue £840.4M, 70+ licences, 65% of transfers instant (from 49% three years earlier). Three products: Account, Business, Platform. Wise says it cannot reach hundreds of millions “by merely requiring them to download the Wise app.” Platform embeds into Nubank, Monzo, Qonto, Brex, Morgan Stanley, Standard Chartered, Itaú. — [Wise FY2025 results](https://wise.com/imaginary-v2/images/7225a78f5d177b9bba2c8152e664ee7e-Wiseplc-FY2025.pdf); [Wise Annual Report FY2025](https://wise.com/imaginary-v2/images/ad736416f90791dbb49c7e59f1f45d8d-WiseFY2025AnnualReportAccounts.pdf)
- Sacra (analysis, citing Wise): Wise moved £145.2B in FY2025 across 15.6 million active customers, with a roughly 58 bps cross-border take rate. — [Sacra, Remitly's Payout Routing Moat](https://sacra.com/chat/h/b16777c6-462c-4cd1-86a5-1ca822fafcb9/)

### Inferences
- **The repeating pattern is “one painful flow, then the other side pays.”** PayPal (eBay sellers paid to get paid faster), Venmo (merchants pay at checkout), Cash App (interchange/lending on inflows), Tremendous (businesses pay for bulk), Payoneer/Grey (platforms and freelancers), Wise (banks embed the FX rail), Tipalti (finance teams pay for AP automation), Rippling (HRIS cross-sell), YouTube (PayPal already sat in the payout slot). Loofta’s pay-by-email/@username claim is a consumer-shaped wedge. The side most likely to pay is businesses and platforms sending many small payouts — giveaways, bounties, research incentives, contractor and AI-task payouts. That is the Tremendous / Hyperwallet / Airtm path, not the Venmo consumer-graph path.
- **Tremendous remains the closest structural analog.** Consumer send-by-email → notice repeat B2B senders in the logs → API + bulk → stay self-serve. Loofta should instrument for repeat-sender SQL the same way.
- **TipLink/Fin is the closest Solana analog, and it just pivoted up-stack.** Same founding team went from claim-links (Loofta’s current shape) to a $17M Series A pitched as *high-value enterprise stablecoin payments*. The market that funded claim-links in 2023 funded “move millions instantly to bank accounts” in 2025.

Gusto did not become a chain company; it piloted Zero Hash. Payoneer, Hyperwallet, Privy, Bridge, Helio-via-Shopify, Mercury-via-startup-word-of-mouth all followed it. Loofta’s payment-links API, SDK, and claim links are the embeddable unit.
- **Solana Pay is a cautionary analog, not a role model.** A free, open QR/URL standard did not create a company. The value accrued to Helio (Shopify plugin, then $175M exit), Cash App/Revolut (closed-loop identities adding USDC), and orchestration layers (Bridge, Noah, Iron). An email-addressed claim flow piggybacks on an identifier that already exists — good for distribution, not a proprietary graph. Pix/UPI won because a *central* party ran the directory.
- **Colosseum’s own book is already crowded with Loofta-shaped peers** (Zoneless for marketplace payouts, DashX/Credible for corridors, KAST/Sling/Revtag for identity send). Differentiation has to be the *claim-before-wallet + specific cash-out corridors + sender embed*, not “Solana payments” as a category.

## What was the moat: network/identity graph, licenses, corridor liquidity, compliance, distribution via platforms, brand?

### Takeaway
Durable moats, in rough order: (1) **distribution through other platforms**, being embedded where payees already get paid; (2) **licenses, entities, and compliance** that take years; (3) **closed-loop balances and identity**, so money stays and the handle is the address book; (4) **corridor liquidity and last-mile cash-out**. Brand and UX alone did not protect anyone. Stablecoin rails lower switching costs further, so the defensible layer is the payee relationship or the sender integration, not the transfer. Sacra’s remittance work and Dragonfly’s 2026 commentary both land on the same sentence: the app is not the moat; routing, compliance, and who owns the user are.

### Inferences
- For Loofta, attainable moats in order: (1) **embedded distribution** — default claim/payout widget inside campaign tools, bounty platforms, AI-task platforms, Discord/Telegram bots, and eventually HR/creator payout settings (Helio-on-Shopify; PayPal-in-YouTube; Zero Hash-in-Gusto); (2) **local cash-out corridors** (Nigerian bank via PAJ; US P2P apps) that are painful to replicate; (3) a **claimant graph** — people who claimed once and can be paid again instantly by any sender. Licenses are a long-run moat but expensive for a solo founder; partnering (Bridge-style providers, PAJ, MoneyGram-class ramps) is the near-term substitute, which is also how Airtm and Zebec scaled enterprise.
- Stablecoin rails commoditize the transfer (a16z on 1.5% being competed down; Zoneless advertising $0.002 vs Stripe $2). The defensible layer is either the **payee relationship** (who the recipient trusts to cash out) or the **sender integration** (where the payout is triggered).
- Identity graphs only compound inside a closed loop with balances ($cashtag, Revtag, PayPal email, KAST Tags, Phantom username). Email-as-address is a distribution cheat-code (PayPal 1999, Loofta today) but not a proprietary namespace. Pix/UPI won because a central bank ran the directory.
- Sacra’s contractor-wallet thesis is the Cash App ladder applied to Loofta’s payees: the first win is the payout; the compounding win is the claimant keeping a balance and being re-payable. Do not pitch the ladder before the first win exists.
- Dragonfly and a16z agree the *issuance* layer is not where a seed payout startup wins. Cards (Rain) and orchestration (Bridge/Mesh) are the funded 2025–26 bets. A claim-link company is closer to Hyperwallet/Tremendous than to Rain.

### Inferences
- Two pricing regimes in 2024–2026. **Strategic stablecoin capability** (Bridge, BVNK, Privy, Helio, and the Rain/Mesh marks) was priced off volume run-rate and buyer urgency. **Mature payout networks** (Payoneer) traded at public-fintech multiples, *below* 2021 SPAC value. A startup is acquirable at a premium if it fills a gap an incumbent cannot build fast: licenses, a wallet base, merchant plugins, or corridors.
- **Helio is the most relevant Solana exit:** ~2.5-year-old Solana payments company, distributed through Shopify/Discord/Woo plugins, sold for $175M on $1.5B *cumulative* volume. Value was integrations and merchants, not proprietary rails. Solana Pay the protocol captured none of that.
- **Acquirer list for a Loofta-type company** (inferred from who bought): Stripe/Bridge (wallets, Valora team, contractor payouts), MoonPay (Solana checkout + Iron API + Agents), Mastercard/Visa (B2B payouts, Visa Direct), Coinbase/Base (Utopia team), Nuvei/Payoneer-type payout networks, Gusto/YouTube-class platforms that already have a payout slot, possibly PAJ-type regional ramps. They paid for **volume, integrations, and compliant cash-out coverage**, not consumer users per se.
- Payoneer at 2.6x sales is the *floor* analog if Loofta ever looks like a licensed cross-border payout network. Bridge at 100x is the *ceiling* analog if Loofta looks like scarce orchestration that Stripe cannot wait to build. Helio at $175M on plugin distribution is the *realistic mid* analog for a Solana payments product with merchants and volume but no licenses.
- Incumbents are already adding the *option* Loofta would sell (YouTube PYUSD, Gusto USDC, Stripe marketplace USDC payouts in preview, Cash App USDC). The remaining gap is **pay-before-account for people who are not already on PayPal/Gusto/Cash App** — long-tail senders and unregistered recipients.

## Which visions are investor-credible in 2026 for stablecoin payouts vs overused?

### Takeaway
Credible 2026 framings are anchored in **observed, measurable payment flows**: B2B and cross-border payouts, contractor/workforce payouts, emerging-market dollar access, and C2B stablecoin payments (the fastest-growing segment). Multicoin wants specialized stablecoin fintechs; Dragonfly is funding cards, interoperability, and cross-border, and says value accrues to whoever owns users, flows, and compliance. “Agent payroll” as a *headline* is acquirer-press language; as a *feature* that lets AI platforms pay humans, it is already a Bridge/Airtm/Outlier flow.

## What did failures look like and why?

### Takeaway
Failures cluster into four modes: (1) **valuation built on a bull market plus a dependency on an acquirer** (Wyre); (2) **FX/treasury exposure in volatile corridors** (Chipper 2022–23 — later survived by cutting to FCF-positive); (3) **compliance and founder-conduct liabilities** that make remediation uneconomic (Paxful); (4) **infra or consumer-P2P products without a durable paying buyer**, which pivot, get acqui-hired, or quietly rebrand (Utopia Labs → Coinbase, Valora → Stripe, TipLink → Fin). Several names in the brief are *not* failures: Request Finance is a going concern past $1B volume; Zebec is live with a Sep 2026 MoneyGram off-ramp; Sendwave paused a Kenya stablecoin *wallet*, not the remittance business.

Protocol fees are storage-level, not a take-rate on payments, so REQ-token “volume = revenue” narratives overstate capture. — [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/request-finance-hits-1-billion-in-bill-payments-secures-strategic-funding-to-scale-stablecoins--fiat-finance-302405617.html); [Request Finance in Numbers](https://www.request.finance/post/request-finance-in-numbers-january-2023); [Request FAQ](https://docs.request.network/faq)
- **Zebec — not a failure.** Live docs/SDKs; Sep 2026 MoneyGram off-ramp on Stellar payroll; self-reported “hundreds of millions” annual payroll volume; “several” employers “in the process of onboarding” (pipeline language). Originally Solana streaming payroll, now multichain + cards. The only “failure case study” previously retrieved ([ReadySetLaunch](https://readysetlaunch.ai/cases/failures/zebec/)) reads as unsourced generated analysis; do not use it. — [Zebec/MoneyGram](https://zebec.io/blog/zebec-and-moneygram-expand-enterprise-payroll-with-global-fiat-off-ramp-access-on-the-stellar-network); [Zebec docs](https://docs.zebec.io/zebec-application-suite/web-superapp/streaming)
- **Coinbase–BVNK** fell through after exclusivity. Even hot deals collapse in diligence. — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/11/11/coinbase-bvnk-2-billion-deal-falls-through-stablecoins/)
- **Solana Pay-as-company** is a non-failure of a different kind: the protocol persisted, the Labs repo was archived (Jan 2025), and economic value moved to Helio / Cash App / orchestration firms. A standard without a paying buyer is not a company. — [Spark](https://www.spark.money/research/solana-pay-protocol-merchant-adoption)

### Inferences
- Lessons for Loofta:
  - (a) **Do not hold local-currency float** (Chipper). Settle in USDC; use partner ramps (PAJ, MoneyGram-class) that take FX risk.
  - (b) **Compliance debt compounds.** Anything touching P2P cash-out (Venmo, Cash App, Zelle, Nigerian bank) needs KYC/AML posture before scale, or it becomes a Paxful-style liability. Sendwave’s Kenya wallet pause — after an Oct 2025 launch — is the 2026 version of corridor-compliance risk.
  - (c) A **consumer stablecoin P2P wallet alone** tends to end in an acqui-hire (Valora) or a pivot (TipLink→Fin, Utopia→Coinbase). Sender-side B2B volume is what creates enterprise value.
  - (d) Infra with no committed buyer pivots (Utopia). Valora’s acqui-hire into Stripe is a realistic *floor* for a strong small team on stablecoin P2P — not nothing, not equity value.
  - (e) **Streaming / novel payment UX is not enough.** Zebec survived by adding cards and a MoneyGram last mile, and even then volume is self-reported and logos are pipeline. The product that mattered was cash-out, not per-second streams.
- Request Finance’s survival is the control case: it attached stablecoins to *business AP/invoicing* (a paying buyer) rather than a token-protocol narrative.
- Crowding is a soft-failure mode: Colosseum’s 2026 batch already contains multiple “payouts / Connect alternative / EM neobank / agent payments” companies. Category noise raises the bar for a distinct wedge.

- Chipper’s actual 2025–26 revenue, valuation, and whether “profitable” is FCF or GAAP: not in filings.
- Celo protocol vs Valora app outcomes beyond the Stripe acqui-hire: not researched.
- Zebec audited volume, customer count, and whether “hundreds of millions” is GMV or a single large contract: not disclosed.
- Sendwave Wallet: no primary Zepz volume for the Solana wallet before the Kenya pause.

## Seed / Series A benchmarks for payout startups in 2025–2026: GMV, revenue, take rate, customer count

### Takeaway
2025 stablecoin-payments *seeds* typically disclosed **$100M–$1B+ cumulative TPV** or **multi-million revenue**, with rounds of $5–22M. Cross-sector Series A bars moved up: SVB/Carta commentary puts **median B2B ARR at Series A near $3M in 2024**, and a fintech GP told Carta he now expects **$5–10M ARR**. Loofta at ~$800/month (~$10k ARR) is pre-seed/angel — and is already at the Colosseum check ($250k) layer, not a priced seed. ### Inferences
- **Take rate decides how much volume you need.** B2B orchestration at 10–25 bps needs billions to matter (Bridge earned perhaps $5–12.5M on $5B). Consumer/long-tail payouts with cash-out at 2–6% need far less volume for the same revenue. Loofta’s claim + cash-out sits in the higher take-rate band. $1M/month of payouts at ~3% blended is ~$360K ARR — a plausible *seed-story* threshold (inference, not a published bar). A 2026 *Series A* in this category, if it looks like software, is being judged against $3–10M ARR.
- 2025 seed comps mostly had **experienced payments founders** (Adyen, Arcus/Mastercard, Citadel→TipLink) or **fast TPV ramps**. A solo founder at ~$800/month should expect pre-seed/angel or the Colosseum $250k layer, *or* the Tremendous path: stay lean, find the B2B sender segment in the logs, grow on revenue.
- TipLink’s 2023 seed on “tens of thousands of links” is the last clean example of a *vision* seed in this exact product shape. Its 2025 Series A required a rebrand and an enterprise-payments story. Do not assume the 2023 bar still exists.
- Competing on “cheaper than Stripe Connect” (Zoneless) is a crowded Colosseum-peer move and collapses take rate. Loofta’s cash-out take (Venmo/Cash App/NGN) is the higher-margin wedge if compliance holds.

## Relevance of AI agents paying humans and humans getting paid by AI platforms (x402, agent wallets, AI data labor) as a vision axis

### Takeaway
Measured honestly, **agent-to-service payments over x402 are real but tiny**: roughly $0.7–1.1M per month of organic volume, about 91 businesses earning meaningful revenue, heavily concentrated. That is a feature or option, not a company thesis, in 2026. The stronger, already-validated axis is **AI platforms paying humans**: data labelers, evaluators, and task workers in emerging markets. The labeling industry is about $2.3B in 2025–26; Outlier/Scale claims $500M+ paid to experts and sits on a parent that hit $1B annualized revenue; Surge/DataAnnotation reported $1.2B revenue in 2024. Bridge’s growth story cited this flow explicitly; Airtm is an official Outlier rail; Gusto is piloting USDC contractor payouts. Keep x402 as a trigger, not the headline.

### Inferences
- **Size check:** all organic x402 commerce (~$1M/month) is roughly what a single mid-sized payout customer might send. Positioning Loofta *primarily* as agent payments would put it in a market where the median seller earns cents, against Flovia/MoonPay Agents/Coinbase facilitator share. Keep x402 as a distribution hook (AI platforms and agents can *trigger* human payouts via Loofta) rather than the headline.
- **The measured vision axis:** “When AI platforms need to pay humans anywhere (labelers, evaluators, bounty solvers, creators), Loofta lets them pay by email or handle, and the human cashes out locally.” Grounded in an acquirer-validated flow (Bridge), uses Loofta’s claim-before-wallet and Nigeria/US cash-out, and offers an *optional* agent-native trigger (x402/SDK). Metrics to show: number of AI or task platforms sending, payouts per month, repeat rate, and percentage claimed to local cash.
- The $2.3B labeling *industry* is not all addressable payout GMV — it includes software, QA, and in-house teams. The worker-payout slice that is on PayPal/Airtm/ACH today is the actual TAM. Outlier’s $500M+ lifetime paid-to-experts and its listed rails (PayPal, Airtm, ACH) are the closest proof that this money already moves through claim-style and EM-cash-out providers. Loofta’s wedge is “pay an email before the worker has an Airtm/PayPal account,” plus NGN cash-out — i.e., the workers and senders *not* already inside Scale/Gusto/YouTube.
- Risk: AI-labor platforms have public contractor complaints about late or missing pay and sudden project cuts (Scale’s 500-contractor cut; Outlier Trustpilot 1-star cluster; Business Insider on post-Meta activity drop). Reliability of payout is the product. Do not cite individual complaint threads as market-size evidence.
- Sacra’s contractor-wallet thesis applies here: the prize after the first AI-task payout is the claimant balance (re-payable, spendable, cash-outable). That is how Airtm turned a remittance wallet into an enterprise AI-workforce rail.
