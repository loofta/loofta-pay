# Stablecoin payout, orchestration, wallet and ramp infrastructure (2025–2026), with fees

Research date: 2026-10-13. Figures are date-stamped where the source dates them. **B2B** = price charged to the integrating business. **Consumer** = fee the end user sees. Unpublished prices are marked **unknown / sales-quoted**. Do not treat aggregator round-ups as list prices.

Source-quality note: comparison sites that also sell competing products (Spark, eco.com, Lumx, BlindPay, Upshift, checkthat.ai, rfp.wiki, Paymentscan, Gate Research, CoinLaw, FoxReload, Payblis) are used only when they quote a number, and are labelled **aggregator**. Prefer vendor pages, OCC/SEC/HKEX filings, and company press releases.

Who these vendors sell to, in one line: Bridge/Stripe, BVNK/Mastercard, Conduit, Sphere, Zero Hash, Borderless, Infinite, Circle CPN/Tazapay, and Bitso Business sell **licensed or KYB’d platforms and enterprises**. MoonPay, Transak, Ramp, Banxa, Mercuryo, Alchemy Pay sell **consumer ramps embedded in apps**, plus a growing enterprise/orchestration layer. Coinbase sells **developers (CDP) and SMBs (Coinbase Business)**. Privy, Dynamic, Crossmint, Circle Wallets, Squads sell **wallet infrastructure to apps**. Rain sells **card issuing to fintechs**; KAST, RedotPay, ether.fi Cash sell **consumer cards**. Yellow Card, Kotani, PAJ, Airtm sell **emerging-market cash-out**. Peer/zkp2p is **permissionless protocol + merchant checkout**, not a licensed ramp. Daimo, Halliday, and Glide (now MoonPay) sell **deposit/orchestration SDKs** that hide chain and rail choice from the end user.

## Exact fees: ramp spreads by method, payout APIs, orchestration bps, wallet MAU pricing, card issuing

### Takeaway
B2B dollar movement on licensed orchestration is about 0–50 bps plus pass-through rails (ACH near $0, wires about $15–30), with almost no vendor publishing a live rate card. Consumer card on-ramps are 2.5–5.5% plus spread; bank on-ramps are about 0.5–1.4%. Wallet infra is cents per MAU ($0.012–$0.05). Issuers (Paxos 0, Brale 0 bps, Circle mint 1:1 for qualified institutions) have pushed mint/redeem toward zero, so margin now sits in distribution, FX, and last-mile cash-out.

### Cited Findings

**Orchestration / payout APIs (B2B)**
- Bridge (Stripe) has no public price list on its docs; developer-fee configuration is public, platform take is not. Platforms may add a `developer_fee_percent` or (beta) `fee_config` with flat plus per-rail fees (`wire`, `ach_push`, `spei`). Fees are withheld from the customer transaction and paid monthly on the 5th in USD. — [Bridge developer fees](https://apidocs.bridge.xyz/platform/orchestration/fees-and-mins/devfees)
- **Conflict on Bridge’s own take:** eco.com (citing a May 2026 read of bridge.xyz/pricing / a developer agreement) says **0.25% basic orchestration and 0.50% virtual-account orchestration**, gas at cost, SWIFT typically $15–$30. — [eco.com (aggregator)](https://eco.com/support/en/articles/15083178-bridge-xyz-stablecoin-api-for-payouts-and-orchestration). Spark cites ~**10 bps** plus network fees (page states “as of mid-2026”). Neither 10 bps nor 25/50 bps is confirmed on a live Bridge-owned pricing page as of this pass. Treat as **10–50 bps, negotiated**. — [Spark orchestration comparison (aggregator)](https://www.spark.money/tools/crypto-payment-orchestration-comparison)
- BVNK: no public price list. Spark (aggregator) puts it at **10–100 bps** tiered. Lumx (aggregator, read 2026-10-08) says BVNK only works with businesses processing at least **$500,000/month** with six months of trading history. — [Spark](https://www.spark.money/tools/crypto-payment-orchestration-comparison); [Lumx](https://lumx.io/blog-posts/stablecoin-api-pricing-compared)
- Conduit: official docs describe a client-level **`take_rate`** model (percentage of the transaction) returned on each quote as `spreadBps` / `effectiveRate`. The bps number itself is **not published**. Marketing: “Competitive, tiered pricing based on volume”; production trial with no platform fees. Money-transmission fees and FX are disclosed per transfer in the MTA, not published. — [Conduit quote pricing](https://docs.conduit.financial/core-concepts/quotes/pricing); [Conduit](https://conduitpay.com/); [Conduit MTA](https://conduitpay.com/mta-terms)
- Sphere (SpherePay): no published rate card. Official API examples show a **fee object split into integrator + platform**. Virtual-account (on-ramp) example: integrator **100 bps** + platform **30 bps** = 130 bps total. Quote API lets the integrator set `integratorBpsFeeRate` (0–10000). For MXN/SPEI→USD/wire or USD/SWIFT quotes, docs say the field “may be omitted or must be 45; omission defaults to 45.” These are **documentation examples / corridor defaults, not a published list price**. — [Sphere virtual account](https://docs.spherepay.co/api-reference/virtual-account/post); [Sphere quote](https://docs.spherepay.co/api-reference/quote/post)
- Zero Hash: no published platform take. Official docs pass through issuer mint/burn fees 1:1. **USDC burn (sell) = 5 bps**; USDC mint = 0; DAI/TUSD/USDT/USDP/PYUSD/RLUSD mint and burn = 0. Platforms can add RFQ fees as bps, notional, or tranche (tier/progressive). — [Zero Hash issuer fees](https://docs.zerohash.com/docs/issuer-fees); [Zero Hash spreads and fees](https://docs.zerohash.com/docs/spreads-and-fees)
- Borderless, Infinite: **unknown / sales-quoted**. Borderless markets a flat platform fee plus pass-through partner rates. — [BlindPay (aggregator)](https://blindpay.com/resources/more/best-stablecoin-apis-2026); [Upshift (2026-10-06)](https://www.upshift.finance/blog/stablecoin-payout-apis)
- Brale (published, B2B): $0/month entry; **0 bps** on standard money movement; USDT conversions **5 bps**; ACH **$0.25**; RTP **$2**; wire **$20**; on-chain at gas + 20%. Mini issuance $10; Pro $500/month with a 90/10 reserve-rewards split. — [Brale pricing](https://brale.xyz/pricing)
- Squads Grid (Solana stablecoin accounts): Basic free up to 1k MAUs; Pro **$499/month** up to 5k MAUs; Enterprise custom. ACH/SEPA/wire ramps, virtual accounts and KYC are **Enterprise only**. — [Squads Grid pricing](https://squads.mintlify.app/grid/v1/pricing)
- Circle Payments Network (CPN): two fee layers — (a) Beneficiary FIs charge local disbursement + FX spread; (b) CPN charges “a variable basis-point charge, tiered by country group.” Gas extra. Chains as of 2025-11-06: Ethereum, Polygon, Solana; Arc coming. No public bps schedule. White paper adds that 1P/3P marketplace fees will come later. Monetization starts H2 2026 (Circle Q2 2026). — [Circle CPN field guide](https://www.circle.com/blog/circle-payments-network-cpn-a-concise-field-guide-for-prospective-network-participants); [CPN white paper](https://6778953.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na1.net/hubfs/6778953/PDFs/Whitepapers/CPN%5FWhitepaper.pdf); [Circle Q2 2026 SEC exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1876042/000187604226000246/augustepr-circle_q22026f.htm)
- Stripe Connect stablecoin payouts: private preview for US platforms, USDC to individuals/sole proprietors (not companies); recipients link a wallet in Express Dashboard. Country count **conflicts**: 67 countries excluding NY and HI vs Spark’s “70+ via Bridge.” — [Upshift](https://www.upshift.finance/blog/stablecoin-payout-apis); [Spark](https://www.spark.money/tools/crypto-payment-orchestration-comparison)

### Inferences
- The stack has split in two. **Issuance and on-chain movement** are racing to 0–25 bps (Brale 0, Paxos 0, Circle mint 1:1, Coinbase 0% USDC for approved apps). **Consumer card ramps and last-mile FX** still charge 1–5%.
- Coinbase’s 0% USDC ramp is an approval-gated subsidy, not a list price.
- Wallet cost is immaterial per payout. At $0.05/MAU, one $20 claim is 0.25% of volume. Privy’s $2,000 PAYG base above 10k MAU is the real fixed cost.
- Yellow Card’s Nigeria **payout** at flat NGN 100 (~$0.06–0.07 at mid-2026 NGN) is far cheaper than its collection fee. If PAJ is in that band, Loofta’s NGN cash-out COGS is a few cents plus PAJ’s unpublished spread, not a 1% rail.
- Sphere’s 30 bps platform-fee *example* and Zero Hash’s 5 bps USDC-burn pass-through are the two most concrete official B2B numbers in a market that otherwise hides take-rates behind sales quotes.

## Licensing footprint and what a non-licensed builder can do

### Takeaway
The acquired leaders now hold or are pursuing federal charters, broad MTLs, and MiCA CASP/EMI. Their APIs let an unlicensed app put money movement in front of users while the vendor is the regulated counterparty and runs KYC. The trade-off: the vendor owns the customer relationship, and most products require that customer to be KYC’d. Peer and Privy pre-generated wallets are the main paths that do **not** require the builder to become a money transmitter — as long as the builder does not take custody of fiat or pool customer funds.

**What this lets a non-licensed builder do**
- Embed a licensed ramp widget (MoonPay, Transak, Ramp, Banxa, Coinbase Onramp): the vendor is the MSB/CASP, runs KYC, and is the counterparty. The builder typically cannot hold the fiat float.
- Call a licensed orchestration API (Bridge, BVNK, Conduit, Zero Hash, CPN via a member FI): the vendor or the member FI is the transmitter. Most require KYB of the platform and KYC of the end customer, plus volume minimums (BVNK $500k/month per Lumx).
- Use Zero Hash / Crossmint “licensed infrastructure” to accept under *their* licenses rather than the builder’s. Fireblocks contrast: Fireblocks Network is “you accept under your own licenses”; Bridge/Zero Hash are “accept under ours.” — [Fireblocks payin comparison](https://www.fireblocks.com/report/compare-payins)
- Use non-custodial wallets (Privy, Dynamic, Crossmint, Squads) and send on-chain USDC: no MTL required for the builder if keys are user-controlled and no fiat is taken.
- Use Peer/zkp2p: protocol does not custody; the **human maker who sells USDC for Venmo** may still be a money-services business under FinCEN if done as a business. That is a legal risk for LPs, not a license the protocol sells.
- PAJ: the builder sends USDC to a PAJ address; PAJ converts and pays a Nigerian bank. The builder is not the naira transmitter; PAJ is.

### Inferences
- “Use Bridge/MoonPay/Coinbase and you do not need an MTL” is true only for the **embedded, vendor-as-principal** pattern. The moment Loofta holds pooled USDC pending claim in a Loofta-controlled key, or converts fiat itself, the analysis changes. Loofta’s unregistered path *does* hold USDC on a Loofta-generated burner whose secret Loofta encrypts — that is closer to hosted-wallet than to Privy-pregenerate-and-user-owns.
- Stripe now owns both the wallet (Privy) and the orchestration (Bridge) plus a path to a federal trust charter and an EU EMI. A Privy-based app can later add Bridge developer fees and Stripe Issuing cards without changing vendors — and is then on a single strategic stack.
- Ramp’s Dec 2025 MTL blog is unusually honest: in unlicensed states it **relied on third-party intermediaries**. That is the same pattern Transak is still in for ~39 states. “160-country coverage” on a marketing page is not the same as the vendor holding the license.
- Zero Hash is the deepest *published* US footprint (BitLicense + near-nationwide MTLs). Bridge’s 33-state count (Fireblocks, Dec 2025 licences page) is narrower; the OCC trust charter is the intended fix.

## Who sells “pay an email/phone, recipient claims later” vs requiring a wallet or a KYC’d customer

### Takeaway
True pay-before-wallet is rare. The closest **API** products are **Crossmint** (send to email/phone/X, wallet auto-created) and **Privy** (pregenerate a Solana/EVM wallet for an email and fund it before first login — Loofta already uses this). **Coinbase Business** and **Coinbase Wallet claim links** do pay-to-email, but the recipient must create a Coinbase account. Most orchestration (Bridge, BVNK, Conduit, CPN, Stripe Connect, Kotani) needs a KYC’d customer, a linked wallet, or a licensed institution on the receive side.

### Cited Findings
- **Privy (Loofta’s wallet):** “You can even send assets to the wallet before the user logs in.” Create a user with `linked_accounts: [{ type: 'email', address }]` and `wallets: [{ chain_type: 'solana' }]`. Supports Ethereum and Solana pregeneration; server wallets for backend-controlled fleets. — [Privy pregenerate](https://docs.privy.io/recipes/pregenerate-wallets); [Privy create user](https://docs.privy.io/user-management/migrating-users-to-privy/create-or-import-a-user)
- **Crossmint:** `wallet.send({ email })` / `{ phone }` / `{ x }` / `{ userId }`. “Sending to email/phone automatically creates a wallet if they don't have one”; recover via OTP. Checkout: email signup; KYC at higher payout tiers. — [Crossmint SDK concepts](https://crossmint-crossmint-sdk.mintlify.app/concepts/wallets); [eco.com (aggregator)](https://eco.com/support/en/articles/14895614-stablecoin-payment-processor-fees-compared-2026)
- **Coinbase Business (Oct 2025):** send USDC to any on-chain address **or email**. “If your recipient doesn’t have a crypto wallet, they can receive the payment via email, create a Coinbase account for free to claim their funds, and easily cash out in their local market.” API supports on-demand, batch, scheduled payouts. US alpha at launch. — [Coinbase Business blog](https://www.coinbase.com/blog/introducing-a-powerful-suite-of-business-payment-tools-on-coinbase-business)
- **Coinbase Wallet “Send via link” (consumer):** Base USDC, ETH on Base, Polygon USDC/MATIC. Recipient must download Coinbase Wallet. Unclaimed funds return after **two weeks**; claim is gasless. — [Coinbase claim links](https://www.coinbase.com/wallet/claim)
- **Peer Pay:** “Fund a payout from the crypto you hold. The recipient signs in with their email and withdraws to the payment app they already use, or to a wallet.” Merchant is the customer; recipient claims later. — [Peer Pay](https://pay.peer.xyz/)
- **Stripe Connect stablecoin payouts:** recipient must **link a wallet** in Express Dashboard; individual/sole proprietor only. — [Upshift](https://www.upshift.finance/blog/stablecoin-payout-apis)
- **Bridge:** customers, virtual accounts, liquidation addresses; funds “transferred by Bridge.” Not an email-claim product. — [Bridge developer fees](https://apidocs.bridge.xyz/platform/orchestration/fees-and-mins/devfees)
- **Kotani:** needs “a customer record for the recipient” plus funded payout balance. — [Kotani offramp](https://developers.kotanipay.com/v3/flows/offramp-flow)
- **PAJ:** standing Solana address tied to a **registered Nigerian bank account**. Anything sent there pays out in naira. Optional BVN on one-off orders. This is pay-an-address-that-is-a-bank, not pay-an-email. — [PAJ offramp](https://docs.paj.cash/concepts/offramp)
- **Peer protocol:** users can connect with “wallet, email, or socials.” — [Peer FAQ](https://peer.xyz/faq)
- **Dynamic:** pre-generated wallets count toward MAU, i.e. it also supports create-before-login. — [Dynamic pricing](https://www.dynamic.xyz/pricing)
- **Daimo:** email OTP + embedded wallet for fiat deposits; “a hint can skip the email or phone entry step, but it cannot skip verification.” This is deposit-in, not pay-an-unregistered-recipient. — [Daimo fiat](https://docs.daimo.com/guides/fiat)
- **Halliday / Glide / Request:** destination is a wallet or a quote; no email-claim product found.
- **Loofta (code-verified, 2026-10-13):** registered users are paid **directly** to their Privy Solana wallet (no burner, no $0.10). Unregistered email / username / X / Discord / GitHub / Telegram get a one-time encrypted burner + claim link. Guest send is live. Recipient later claims to own wallet, external address, or PAJ Naira default bank. — [loofta-tech-inventory.md](loofta-tech-inventory.md)

### Inferences
- Loofta’s differentiator is not “pay an email” alone — Privy, Crossmint, and Coinbase Business already do that. It is **pay @username/email, recipient later chooses USDC or Venmo/Cash App/Revolut/Zelle (Peer) or NGN bank (PAJ)**, on Solana, with MagicBlock privacy and NEAR Intents for cross-chain deposit. No listed infra vendor packages that multi-rail claim-time choice.
- Coinbase Business is the most serious **product** competitor for “email payout + later cash-out,” but it forces the recipient into a Coinbase account and was US-alpha at launch. Loofta can stay wallet-optional and corridor-specific (Nigeria, US P2P apps).
- PAJ’s standing Solana address is the right primitive **after** a recipient has claimed once and registered a bank. First payout still needs an email/username hold.
- Almost every licensed orchestrator is “pay a KYC’d customer or a wallet address.” The unlicensed-builder-friendly claim-later products are wallets (Privy/Crossmint/Dynamic) plus Coinbase’s closed account, not Bridge/BVNK/CPN.

### Gaps
- Sphere, Infinite, Airtm, Bitso Business claim-link features not verified.
- Whether Bridge or Stripe Connect will add native email-claim (vs wallet-link) after the Privy acquisition is unknown.
- Unclaimed-payment expiry and refund rules for Loofta vs Coinbase’s two-week clawback were not compared in production.

## Where Loofta’s stack sits, and fee headroom at 1–5%

### Takeaway
Loofta is a **consumer distribution / claim app**, not an orchestrator. Variable COGS per payout is low: Solana gas <<$0.01, Privy ~$0.05/MAU (or $0 inside free tiers), Peer 0–0.95% (plus ≤0.5% if bridging) or 0% if the recipient is the Peer Cash maker, PAJ an unpublished NGN spread, MagicBlock 10 bps + 0.002 SOL on private sends. A **1%** Loofta fee is competitive with bank ramps (Ramp 1.4%, Transak SEPA 0.99%, Yellow Card NGN collect 0.5–1%) and far above B2B rails (0–50 bps). **3–5%** matches card on-ramps and Peer Pay’s fiat checkout, and is defensible only as a convenience tax (claim-later, no wallet, last-mile P2P). It is **not** defensible against Coinbase 0% USDC or Bridge/Brale B2B payouts.

### Cited Findings (inputs)

**Loofta stack vs vendors (role, not a price)**
- Wallet: **Privy** (Stripe) — pregenerate + email login. Alternative: Dynamic (Fireblocks), Crossmint, Circle Wallets, Squads. Loofta is a Privy customer, not a competitor.
- US / P2P cash-out: **Peer / zkp2p** (permissionless). Alternative licensed path: Coinbase Offramp (0% USDC to ACH, Coinbase account required), MoonPay/Transak/Ramp off-ramp, Bridge payouts, Airtm.
- Nigeria cash-out: **PAJ Cash**. Alternative: Yellow Card (published NGN 100 flat disbursement), Kotani (quote API).
- Cross-chain in: **NEAR Intents**. Alternatives: Daimo Pay, Halliday, Glide/MoonPay Trade, Request/LiFi, CCTP.
- Private transfers: **MagicBlock**. No listed infra vendor sells this. Loofta live-prices **10 bps volume + 0.002 SOL** relay plus its own **10 bps** private-payment cut. — [loofta-tech-inventory.md](loofta-tech-inventory.md)
- Loofta does **not** currently sit on Bridge, BVNK, CPN, Circle Mint, or a consumer ramp widget for the core claim flow.
- Current Loofta take on the core rail: **$0.10** unregistered-claim fee (frontend-only constant); **10 bps** on private sends; Peer cash-out **2% min $0.30**; PAJ Naira **0–10 bps** tiered; cross-chain deposit **30 bps**. Giveaway 10% is a separate product. — [loofta-tech-inventory.md](loofta-tech-inventory.md)

**Cost and comparable take-rates (sourced above)**
- Solana transfers “less than $0.01 regardless of amount” (vendor claim). — [Mercuryo guide](https://mercuryo.io/explore/learn/cross-border-crypto-payments-business-guide); Circle wallet gas table lists Solana transfer on the order of **$0.006**. — [Circle gas fees](https://developers.circle.com/wallets/gas-fees)
- Privy: $0.05/MAU + $0.01/signature on PAYG above free tiers; $2,000 base above 10k MAU. — [Privy pricing](https://www.privy.io/pricing)
- Peer protocol: ≤0.95% taker + ≤0.5% bridge. Peer Cash maker at 0 bps oracle. Peer Pay merchant: 1% crypto / 2.95–4.95% fiat. — [Peer FAQ](https://peer.xyz/faq); [peer-cash](https://github.com/zkp2p/peer-cash); [Peer Pay](https://pay.peer.xyz/)
- Yellow Card NGN payout: flat NGN 100. Collections 0.5–1%. — [Yellow Card fees](https://help.yellowcard.io/articles/8792393400-supported-payment-methods-and-associated-fees-for-yellowcard-s-payment-api)
- Consumer ramps: Coinbase card 2.5% / ACH 0.5% / USDC 0% approved; MoonPay up to 4.5% (min $3.99–$4.50); Ramp bank 1.40%; Transak official bank 2%+$1 / card 3.99%+$1. — sources in section 1
- B2B rails: Bridge ~10–50 bps (unconfirmed range), BVNK 10–100 bps (aggregator), Sphere example 30 bps platform, Brale 0 bps, Zero Hash USDC burn pass-through 5 bps. — section 1
- Platforms are expected to add their own fee: Bridge `developer_fee_percent`, Request `feePercentage`, Halliday `business_fees`, PAJ `businessUSDCFee`, Ramp partner fee, Sphere `integratorBpsFeeRate`. — [Bridge](https://apidocs.bridge.xyz/platform/orchestration/fees-and-mins/devfees); [Request](https://docs.request.network/api-features/crosschain-payments); [Halliday](https://halliday.mintlify.app/api-reference/payments/get-payment-quotes); [PAJ](https://docs.paj.cash/); [Ramp](https://support.rampnetwork.com/en/articles/10415-what-fees-are-charged-when-buying-crypto); [Sphere quote](https://docs.spherepay.co/api-reference/quote/post)
- World Bank Q2 2024 average cost of sending $200 = **6.65%**; international wires “up to $50.” That is the consumer-remittance ceiling Loofta is substituting, not the B2B orchestration floor. — [CPN white paper](https://6778953.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na1.net/hubfs/6778953/PDFs/Whitepapers/CPN%5FWhitepaper.pdf)

### Inferences (illustrative arithmetic, not sourced prices)
- **$50 claimed to USDC on Solana:** COGS ≈ gas (<$0.01) + Privy ($0 if under 500 MAU; $0.05 if PAYG) ≈ 0.02–0.1%. At **1%** Loofta keeps ~$0.45–0.50; at **3%** ~$1.45; at **5%** ~$2.45. Gross margin >90% on the USDC-claim path.
- **$50 to Venmo via Peer:** if Loofta/recipient is taker, COGS up to 0.95% (+0.5% if bridged off Base). If recipient is Peer Cash maker, protocol fee 0 but fill risk and ~2% counterparty spread exist in the market (DefiLlama maker-spread ≈ 2% of 30-day volume). A **2–3%** Loofta fee leaves ~1–2 points after a 0.95% taker fee and still sits under MoonPay-class card ramps. A **5%** fee plus 0.95% Peer plus any bridge starts to look worse than Coinbase Business 1% + Coinbase cash-out. Loofta already charges **2% min $0.30** on Peer cash-out — that is already in the defensible band.
- **$50 to NGN via PAJ:** PAJ margin is inside `offRampRate`. If PAJ is near Yellow Card’s **flat NGN 100** disbursement, rail COGS is ~0.1% on $50 plus spread. A **2–3%** Loofta fee then has 1.5–2.5 points of headroom even after a 0.5–1% implied spread. If PAJ’s all-in spread is 2%+, a 5% Loofta fee becomes a 7%+ user experience and will lose to Yellow Card-class B2B. Loofta’s current Naira take (0–10 bps) is **well below** the 1–5% strategy question — it is priced like B2B orchestration, not like a consumer ramp.
- **Competitive ceiling:**
  - **1–1.5%** — competes with bank ramps and Coinbase Business payment links (1%); clear headroom vs 0–50 bps B2B cost; the honest price for “I just wanted USDC.”
  - **2–3%** — matches Peer Pay Base fiat and sits under card ramps; justifiable when the recipient uses Venmo/Cash App/Revolut/Zelle or NGN and never opens a wallet.
  - **4–5%** — only for sub-$90 tickets (MoonPay’s $3.99–$4.50 min already exceeds 5% below ~$80–90) or for fully last-mile convenience. Loses immediately to Coinbase 0% USDC and to any user who can receive a wallet address. Still under the World Bank 6.65% remittance average.
- **Vendor positioning:** Bridge/BVNK/Conduit/CPN sell to licensed or KYB’d businesses at volume (BVNK $500k/month). Loofta is a layer above: consumer claim UX + corridor partners (PAJ, Peer) + non-custodial wallet (Privy) + privacy (MagicBlock) + cross-chain in (NEAR Intents). That is a **distribution** business, not an infra business, which is why 1–5% is the consumer-ramp band, not the 10–50 bps orchestration band.
- **Strategic risk:** Stripe (Privy+Bridge+Connect+Issuing) and Coinbase Business can each ship “email payout + cash-out” as a feature of a licensed stack. Circle + Tazapay can ship licensed last-mile in 100 markets. Loofta’s remaining room is (1) pay-before-any-account including @username, (2) claim-time rail choice including Peer + PAJ, (3) Solana + privacy, (4) corridors those two will under-serve (Nigeria first).
- **Do not compete on B2B payout APIs.** A 1–5% Loofta fee on a $10k contractor payout loses to Bridge/Brale/Coinbase Business on price and to BVNK/Tazapay on licenses. The 1–5% question only makes sense on **claim-later consumer tickets**.

### Gaps
- No live measurement of PAJ’s NGN spread vs NAFEX/parallel, or of Peer’s current Venmo/Cash App fill spreads and time-to-fill.
- NEAR Intents app fee is env-gated (backend default 0); live production value not confirmed.
- No data on unclaimed-payment rates or refund COGS.
- No Loofta volume to test whether Peer TVL ($82K) is already a constraint.
- Whether Loofta’s $0.10 unregistered fee is collected on-chain or only shown in the UI is a code-inventory gap, not a market-research gap.
