# Creator payroll vs nano-creator acquisition (pay by @handle)

Honest 2025–2026 assessment for Loofta. Two jobs are routinely collapsed under “creators.” They are not the same SAM, buyer, compliance stack, or retention loop.

**(a) Professional creator payroll:** mid-tier and up, invoices, KYC, 1099/W-8, $1k–$15k per deal, paid by agencies/brands/platforms. Competitors are Gigapay, Tipalti, Trolley, Deel, Toku, Stripe Connect.

**(b) Nano sprays:** $5–$75 to thousands of local/UGC/clipper accounts for a post, clip, or store visit. Competitors are Tremendous (incentives), Whop Content Rewards / CLIPPING.net (clipping marketplaces), Relish (local seeding), Influee/Billo/Insense (UGC production).

Do not use the $32B–$40B influencer-marketing TAM, and especially not the $250B–$480B “creator economy,” as Loofta’s SAM. Loofta’s plausible wedge is the *payout rail* on (b), or white-label rails for marketplaces that already own (a) or (b)—not “we become the creator payroll company.”

## Is paying by @handle a real 2026 job, and is “creators” the right beachhead?

### Takeaway
Paying people identified by a social account is a real, growing 2026 workflow—but it is a **feature of marketplaces and payroll APIs**, not a standalone company. “Creators” is the wrong beachhead if it means competing with Gigapay/Tipalti/Deel for mid-tier invoices. It is a *possible* beachhead only if Loofta treats nano/clipper/UGC payouts as **Tremendous-scale incentives plus claim UX**, sold to the platforms that already source the humans.

### Cited Findings
- Global influencer-marketing spend is cited at **$32.55B in 2025** (Later via PR Newswire) and **$32.6B in 2025** (CreatorIQ). Mordor Intelligence puts 2025 at **$31.07B** and 2026 at **$40.51B**. These are brand *spend on influencer marketing*, not Loofta revenue. — [Later / PR Newswire, 25 Jun 2025](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/influencer-marketing-in-2025-new-data-reveals-what-works-what-costs-and-whats-next-302490369.html); [CreatorIQ, 21 Jan 2026](https://www.creatoriq.com/press/releases/state-of-creator-compensation-); [Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/influencer-marketing-market)
- A separate, much larger “creator economy” figure ($250B in 2023, approaching $480B by 2027; 100M creators by 2030) comes from Goldman Sachs and is repeated by Epidemic Sound. Influencer Marketing Hub’s 2025 earnings report then calls the “global influencer market” a “$250 billion industry,” which conflates creator-economy GMV (ads, merch, subs, courses) with influencer-marketing spend. — [Epidemic Sound Creator Economy Report 2026, citing Goldman Sachs](https://downloads.ctfassets.net/js6ap5wzepad/2EwMABVPOT9ToweqNnqzDP/ea0efbb8fe11a322f53163e9cf9cb2ca/The_Future_of_the_Creator_Economy_Report_2026.pdf); [Influencer Marketing Hub Creator Earnings Report 2025](https://influencermarketinghub.com/creator-earnings-report-2025/)

- SignalFire’s widely cited “50 million creators / 2 million full-time” split is from **September 2020**, not 2025–2026. They call ~$15k the “Monetization Barrier.” 43.8% of respondents had under 100k followers. This is a surveyed (already-platformed) creator set, not the unmonetized long tail. — [IMH Creator Earnings Report 2025](https://influencermarketinghub.com/creator-earnings-report-2025/)
- Later 2025: 73% of brands prefer micro and mid-tier; nano median CPM can reach **$211** vs micro **$119**, with engagement **6.15–6.76%**. - Mordor: **micro creators held 39.35% of influencer-marketing spend in 2025** (~$12.23B of their $31.07B); nano is the fastest-growing type at **34.92% CAGR through 2031**. Agencies adopt “CRM-style tooling to manage hundreds of nano relationships.” — [Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/influencer-marketing-market)
- Professional payroll products already exist for (a): Gigapay needs only **name, email, amount, country**; creators then KYC, pick a rail, and claim. Gigapay is merchant-of-record / self-billing, **€279/mo + 4.9% per payout** (Tipalti’s 2026 comparison). Tipalti Mass Payments from **$249/mo** with W-8/W-9 and 1099. This is email-based payroll, not “pay @handle.” — [Gigapay how it works](https://www.gigapay.com/why-gigapay/how-it-works); [Tipalti creator payouts API, 7 Oct 2026](https://tipalti.com/resources/learn/content-creator-payouts-api/)

### Inferences
- The people who *actually get paid like a job* are a thin slice: CreatorIQ’s top 10% take 62% of brand-payment volume; IMH’s surveyed majority still fails to clear $15k/year. Millions of nano accounts exist; **most of them are not a payroll customer**. They are a cheap media/seeding supply.
- “Pay any @username” is a **claim/routing UX** (Loofta, PaySocial) sitting under someone else’s demand. The companies that make money on creator pay own **demand + compliance** (Whop, Relish, Gigapay, Meta, TikTok), not the alias.
- Moving Loofta’s story from crypto giveaways → campaign rewards → “creator payouts (discovery → deals → pay by handle)” stacks three products: a marketplace, a deal desk, and a rail. The rail is the only piece that matches Loofta’s current send/claim/off-ramp. Discovery and deals are Relish/Whop/CreatorIQ’s job.
- Beachhead ranking for Loofta, given existing product (pay handle/email, claim without a wallet): **(1) payout rail for clipping/UGC/seeding marketplaces and research incentives in PayPal-weak geos; (2) not mid-tier creator payroll; (3) not “invent quantum creators and keep them as Venmo users.”**

### Gaps
- No 2025–2026 primary census of how many people received *any* brand payment last year (CreatorIQ is platform-biased toward brands that already pay through software).
- SignalFire has not published an updated 50M/2M split for 2025–2026 in sources found here.
- eMarketer’s “$21B creators pocket” and “45.5% nano+micro” sit behind a paywall; only the public teaser was available.
- IAB’s US creator-ad-spend figure cited in a truncated Business Insider snippet was not independently confirmed; do not use it.

## What do nano / UGC / clippers actually get paid? ($ amounts)

### Takeaway
True nano work in 2026 is **$50–$500 per branded post** on rate cards, **$50–$75 in live Relish-style local campaigns**, **$50–$150 (often ~$150 typical) for a short UGC video**, and **about $1 per 1,000 views for clipping** (commonly $0.20–$1.50). A typical clipper or local seeder is in **Tremendous / Swagbucks money**, not Gigapay invoice money. UGC production rates are being compressed (one 2026 source: −44% YoY).

### Cited Findings
- 2026 rate-card benchmarks (agency/aggregator, not invoices): **nano (1k–10k) $50–$500 per post**; micro (10k–100k) $200–$5,000; mid-tier $1,200–$15,000. Janney’s Q4 2024 scrape of 180M+ profiles: nano TikTok **$50–$200**, IG **$50–$250**. — [Gigapay blog, 2026 rates](https://www.gigapay.com/blog/how-much-to-pay-influencers-in-2026); [Janney AI 2025 benchmarks](https://www.janney.ai/blog/influencer-benchmark-2025/)
- UGCagent’s 2026 model (index of 26,791 UGC-qualified creators, **median ~1,700 followers**): nano 15–30s video **$150 typical ($100–$250)**; a 5k-follower IG post or TikTok **$60 typical**. 3-month paid usage +$40; 12-month usage equals another ~$150. — [UGCagent nano rates](https://ugcagent.app/ugc-rates/nano-influencer-rates/); [UGCagent UGC rates 2026](https://ugcagent.app/ugc-rates/)
- Marketplace UGC: Billo **$99–$300+** per video; Insense **$100–$300+** UGC and **$125+** nano post; Influee **~$22–$77+** per 30-second video. inBeat 2026: average **$212**, median **$150**. LaunchPoint: average UGC deliverable **$198, down 44% YoY**. Short simple video often **$50–$150**. — [FindClout UGC rates 2026](https://findclout.com/blog/ugc-creator-rates-2026)
- Clipping (pay-per-1,000-views, clipper posts on *their* account): going rate **~$1 / 1,000 views**; published range **$0.20–$1.50**, some campaigns **$3–$6**. Whop Content Rewards: brand sets CPM, **10% brand fee**, campaigns from **$1,000**; clippers pay a cut of CPM. CLIPPING (the agency) site **$0.20–$0.75**; Bloomberg (Oct 2025, cited by viral.app) **$0.30–$1.50**; clients **$2,500–$10,000/month**. Streamer/casino campaigns often **$0.40–$0.80**. — [viral.app clipping campaigns 2026](https://viral.app/blog/guides/clipping-campaigns); [ClipAffiliates CPM 2026](https://www.clipaffiliates.com/blog/clipping-cpm-rates-2026)
- Relish (local CPG/restaurant seeding, PayPal): live campaign pages pay **$50** (Man vs Fries, MrBeast Burger: $25 on schedule + $25 after post) to **$75** (Pricklee: $15 pickup + $60 after post, minus $3–$4 PayPal fee). FTC disclosure required (“Partnered with …”). This is the empirical “quantum creator” check size. — [Relish Pricklee campaign](https://tryrelish.com/campaign/pricklee-adventure-make-it-part-of-your-everyday-r); [Relish Man vs Fries](https://tryrelish.com/campaign/man-vs-fries-your-ultimate-summer-cravings-showdow); [Relish MrBeast Burger](https://tryrelish.com/campaign/mrbeast-burger-fueling-summer-cravings-with-bold-f)
- TikTok Creator Rewards (ex-Creativity Program) is **not a nano program**: **10,000 authentic followers + 100,000 views in 30 days**, original videos **≥1 minute**, ~**$0.40–$1.00 per 1,000 qualified views** (creator-reported; TikTok does not publish RPM). Minimum payout **$10 or $50** depending on which TikTok page you trust. Paid/sponsored content is **ineligible**. Platform-native pay therefore excludes the 1k–10k nano band Loofta would “invent.” — [TikTok Creator Rewards terms](https://www.tiktok.com/legal/page/global/tiktok-creator-rewards-program-eea/en); [Gelato 2026 TikTok pay](https://www.gelato.com/blog/how-much-does-tiktok-pay)
- CLIPPING.net pays clippers in **PayPal, USDC on Ethereum, or USDT on Ethereum** after campaign end and sponsor approval—not instant, not Solana. — [CLIPPING payments docs](https://clipping.net/docs/clippers/payments)

### Inferences
- A clipper who gets 20,000 verified views at $1 CPM earns **$20**. Relish pays **$50–$75** for a local Reel. Influee can pay **under $80** for a 30s UGC clip. These are **research-incentive / gig** amounts. Calling the recipients “creators” in the CreatorIQ/Gigapay sense is a category error.
- Rate-card nano ($50–$500) is the *asking* range for a sponsored post from someone with 1k–10k followers. Transactional seeding/clipping/UGC production is cheaper and more automated. Loofta’s “tiny amounts to engage with brands” maps to the second, not the first.
- Compression at the commodity UGC end (−44% YoY in one dataset; Influee at $22–$77) means brands can already buy faces/volume without a new “quantum creator” class. Supply is abundant (UGCagent: 26k creators, median 1.7k followers).
- Arithmetic for Loofta SAM: even if nano+micro get 45.5% of ~$40B 2026 influencer spend (~$18B), almost all of that is already paid via agency wire, PayPal, Stripe, Relish, Whop, Insense. The uncaptured slice is **cross-border small-dollar claim** where PayPal fails—not “the creator economy.”

### Gaps
- No independent 2026 dataset of *median annual income* for clippers or Relish-type seeders (campaign pages show per-job pay, not yearly).
- Bloomberg’s clipping-agency article (Oct 2025) was cited second-hand; full article not fetched.
- TikTok’s official RPM is unpublished; $0.40–$1.00 is third-party/creator-reported.

## Who already pays by handle or social ID — is “pay @handle” a feature or a company?

### Takeaway
**Feature, not a company.** Platforms pay *their* creators (Meta USDC, TikTok Rewards, X Money). Marketplaces pay *their* campaign supply (Whop, Relish, CLIPPING, UGC tools) after the human is already a member. Storefronts (Fourthwall, Beacons, Fanbasis/Commas) pay *their* sellers via Stripe/PayPal. The only products that look like Loofta’s “send to @user, they claim” are **PaySocial-class aliases** and **Tremendous-class email claims**—payout UX, not a creator business.

### Cited Findings
- **Meta USDC (2026):** select Facebook/Instagram creators in **Argentina, Colombia, Peru, Philippines** receive USDC on **Solana or Polygon** via Stripe; wallet linking; tax docs from Meta and Stripe. Stripe’s Link head: “We’re already partnering with Meta so their creators can receive stablecoins in their Link wallets in countries like the Philippines and Colombia.” This is **platform payroll to existing creators**, not pay-any-stranger-by-handle. — [Meta Business Help](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1141348158001625); [CoinDesk, 29 Apr 2026](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/04/29/tech-giant-meta-starts-paying-some-creators-in-stablecoin-with-stripe-s-support)
- **Stripe Connect stablecoin payouts:** private preview, **US-based platforms only**, USDC to individuals/sole proprietors, Express Dashboard, Transfers API. Not a public “pay @handle” product. — [Stripe docs](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/stablecoin-payouts)
- **X:** US creator payouts moved **from Stripe to X Money** on 2 Sep 2026 (Original Content Rewards + subscriptions); instant, no $30 minimum / two-week cycle. Non-US stays on Stripe. X is **forcing the identity into its own wallet**, the opposite of paying an external handle. — [TechCrunch, 2 Sep 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/02/x-shifts-us-creator-payouts-from-stripe-to-x-money/)
- **Whop Content Rewards:** brands set budget + **$ per 1,000 views**; creators submit post URLs; AI review; auto-approve in 48h; Whop pays from campaign budget. Clipping or UGC. Whop Clips (first-party) claims **up to $190,000/month** paid to clippers; YouTube clips example **$1.25 / 1k views**. Identity is a **Whop account**, not a raw @handle. — [Whop Content Rewards blog](https://whop.com/blog/whop-content-rewards/); [Whop docs](https://docs.whop.com/memberships-and-access/third-party-apps/content-rewards); [Whop Clips](https://whop.com/blog/whop-clips/)
- **Relish (tryrelish.com):** managed local-creator marketplace (60 cities). Relish sources, vets, tracks store visits. Creators get paid **PayPal**; some campaigns still ask the creator to **email their PayPal handle**. Relish is the company; PayPal is the rail; the Instagram account is the *work identity*. — [Relish home](https://tryrelish.com/); [Relish about](https://tryrelish.com/about); Relish campaign pages above
- **CLIPPING.net:** clipper marketplace; payouts PayPal or ETH USDC/USDT after campaign close. — [CLIPPING payments](https://clipping.net/docs/clippers/payments)
- **Fourthwall:** creator merch/storefront. Monthly Stripe Connect to bank/debit, **$25 threshold**. Explicitly **no PayPal, Wise, crypto, or gift-card payouts**. Handle is the shop brand, not the payment address. — [Fourthwall how you get paid](https://help.fourthwall.com/frequently-asked-questions/payments-and-pricing/how-you-get-paid)
- **Beacons.ai:** link-in-bio commerce. Product sales via Stripe/PayPal in real time; **affiliate commissions PayPal-only**, pending **30–90 days**. Bank account “informational only.” — [Beacons payouts](https://help.beacons.ai/en/articles/4700481); [Beacons affiliate commissions](https://help.beacons.ai/en/articles/5870529)
- **Fanbasis (rebranded Commas):** creator community/storefront; payouts ACH, RTP, wire, PayPal, Venmo, Cash App, **crypto**; 100+ countries. Still a **seller platform**, not pay-any-handle. Fees not fully public. — [MoveToWhop comparison](https://movetowhop.com/compare/whop-vs-fanbasis)
- **PaySocial:** closest literal “pay Instagram/X/Google handle as alias,” then recipient sets bank or crypto. This is a **payment-alias company**, not a creator marketplace. — [PaySocial](https://www.paysocial.io/)
- **Reels (usereels.app):** Pump.fun-style coins launched *for* an Instagram username; creator **claims by putting a code in IG bio**—no wallet needed to start. Crypto-native, not brand payroll. — [Reels docs](https://www.usereels.app/docs)
- **Rally.io:** creator-coin / $RLY sidechain product, not USD/USDC payroll. Treat as a 2021-era crypto-creator experiment, not a 2026 cash competitor. — [Rally creator 101](https://wiki.rally.io/rally-io/for-creators/creator-101)
- **Gigapay:** pays by **email after KYC**, one consolidated invoice to the brand. Explicitly not social-handle settlement. — [Gigapay how it works](https://www.gigapay.com/why-gigapay/how-it-works)

### Inferences
- Pattern: whoever **owns the campaign or the audience** owns the payout. Meta/TikTok/X will not let Loofta sit between them and their creators. Whop/Relish/CLIPPING will use Loofta only if cheaper/faster than PayPal/ETH USDC in the geos they cannot serve.
- “Discovery → deals → pay by handle” is Relish + CreatorIQ + Gigapay glued together. Each layer already has a category leader. Loofta entering as the third-layer rail is plausible; Loofta entering as the first two is a different company.
- For Loofta’s existing claim-without-wallet UX, the analogue to copy is **Tremendous (email claim)** and **PaySocial (handle alias)**, not Fourthwall/Beacons (those are storefronts that already have Stripe).

### Gaps
- Relish does not publish take-rate, GMV, or creator count.
- Whop’s “$190k/month to clippers” is a marketing claim about one operator’s budget, not audited GMV.
- Fanbasis/Commas fee schedule is not public.
- No evidence Rally.io is a meaningful 2026 payout venue.

## Gig / affiliate platforms that already do handle-based payouts (Impact, PartnerStack, Gleam)

### Takeaway
Affiliate and ambassador platforms **identify partners with tracking links, not social handles as payment addresses**. They already automate commission → PayPal/ACH/invoice. Gleam automates *social actions for contests*, not payroll. Loofta does not unlock a new affiliate job; at best it is an alternative **disbursement rail** for partners in geos those platforms pay poorly.

### Cited Findings
- **impact.com:** partners get tracking links; optional SharedId / traffic source / subaccount parameters; brands can set **payout rules on those strings**. Advocate participants have cash-payout details, W-9, and a selected payout method inside impact—not “send USDC to @user.” — [impact tracking links](https://help.impact.com/partner/what-would-you-like-to-learn-about/platform-features/tracking/tracking-links/create-and-manage-links/create-tracking-links); [impact CPC V2 FAQs](https://help.impact.com/brand/what-would-you-like-to-learn-about/platform-features/contracts-and-template-terms/template-terms/cpc-v2-faqs); [Advocate participant profiles](https://help.impact.com/brand/what-would-you-like-to-learn-about/advocate-program/manage-advocate-participants/advocate-participant-profiles-explained)
- **PartnerStack:** marketplace of 600+ B2B brands; **automated commission payouts** for affiliate, co-sell, referral, and influencer partners; “single monthly invoice.” Influencers are a partner *type* inside a PRM, not pay-by-handle. — [PartnerStack](https://partnerstack.com/)
- **Beacons affiliate:** brand-set CPA, PayPal cash-out after 30–90 day lock. — [Beacons affiliate](https://help.beacons.ai/en/articles/5870529)
- **Gleam:** SaaS for contests/social actions (follow, tweet, etc.), **$29–$479/month** packages; prizes are typically product/coupons. Gleam Rewards is coupons, not bank/stablecoin. (From adjacent Loofta research: ESL/Kinguin case studies use sponsor hardware, not cash rails.) — Gleam pricing cited in prior notes via [Gleam](https://gleam.io/pricing); not a creator payroll product
- **Whop / CLIPPING / Relish** (see previous section) are the actual “pay many social accounts for posts” stack, and they sit in **performance content**, not classic affiliate CPA.

### Inferences
- Handle-based *attribution* (UTM, SharedId, “posted from @x”) is a solved tracking problem. Handle-based *settlement* is what Loofta sells. Affiliate platforms already settle to registered payees. Switching them to USDC/email-claim only wins where PayPal/ACH is the pain (EM, unbanked clippers)—the same Tremendous/Airtm wedge.
- Gleam ambassadors/contests are **engagement campaigns**, not payroll. Paying contestants $5 to tweet is a sweepstakes/incentive product (legal, tax, fraud) that Gleam deliberately avoids by giving product prizes.

### Gaps
- Did not find a 2025–2026 primary page for “Gleam Ambassadors” as a paid-ambassador payroll product.
- PartnerStack’s influencer payout rail (PayPal vs ACH vs crypto) is not documented in the pages retrieved.
- Impact’s published payout-method list for advocates was not fully fetched.

## Do users acquired via “come for the money” retain as product users? (D30)

### Takeaway
**Be brutal: they do not, unless you keep paying.** Quest/airdrop D30 on-chain retention is typically **under 1%**. Academic quest data shows retention **~0% and incentive-driven**. GPT/cashback users retain **on the rewards product itself** at maybe 15–30% D30 *while offers pay*; they do not convert into unpaid send/claim users. Ibotta’s own D2C app is **declining** and they will not even spend on acquisition unless offer supply is strong. The quantum-creator loop (come for $20, stay for Loofta P2P/off-ramp) has **no supporting 2025–2026 evidence** and strong contrary evidence.

### Cited Findings
- Analysis of quest campaigns from **26 dApps** (Genki, Quest3, Galxe; ETH/BNB; 1k–5k+ participants): **vast majority had ~1% on-chain retention after 30 days**. Exceptions: Solv **16%**, Duet **6%**. A 93.5% “retention” outlier (GoodGhosting) was another incentive around day 30. — [TechFlow / quest retention](https://www.techflowpost.com/en-US/article/11114)
- “Quest Love” academic paper (FinTeAchin 2024): 1.2M unique addresses, 80M quest completions. Between epochs 21–35, retention rate **−0.183%**; after token rewards, **0.59%**. Tx volume fell from 11.3M peak to 572k (5% of peak). Authors: engagement was “transactional and profit-driven.” Participation collapsed after airdrop snapshots (−29.5% then another −42%). — [Allen et al., Quest Love PDF](https://www.pulpspy.com/papers/2024_finteachin.pdf)
- Galxe’s Optimism Quests case study reports **~50% increase in Optimism usage** and daily transacting wallets **11.65k → 25.13k** comparing 30 days pre vs post. That is **ecosystem activity during/after a paid campaign**, not cohort D30 of quest-acquired wallets remaining as unpaid product users. Treat as vendor marketing, not retention proof. — [Galxe help: Optimism](https://help.galxe.com/en/articles/8614334-ecosystem-campaign-optimism)
- Layer3 investor write-up (Alea) flags **“quest fatigue and user-quality decay”** as a core risk even while selling “gamified retention.” — [Alea on Layer3](https://www.alearesearch.io/reports/theses/layer3)
- GPT-site operator benchmarks (not Swagbucks-disclosed): healthy **D30 15–30%**, ARPU **$1–$5/month**, payout ratio **40–60%**, redemption **60–85%**. That D30 is **retention on the GPT site**—users returning to earn more—not conversion to a non-reward product. — [RevBoost GPT guide](https://www.revboost.com/guides/how-to-monetize-gpt-site); [Perkox GPT 2026](https://blog.perkox.com/2025/07/get-paid-to-gpt-websites-mobile-monetization-how-to-dominate-reward-channels-in-2025/)
- Swagbucks economics: users typically **$1–$5/day** with grind; 2025 user reports of **$1–$3/hour** and collapsed search rewards; lifetime earners still frame it as beer money. The product *is* the money. When rates worsen, sentiment collapses (PissedConsumer 1.8★ cited by a critic). — [Andy Zhong Swagbucks 2025 review](https://andyhzhong.com/2025/05/27/swagbucks-2025-2033-70-lifetime-earnings-review/); [Deshittify / Swagbucks](https://www.deshittify.io/products/swagbucks)
- Ibotta FY2026 Q2 earnings: D2C app engagement/ad revenue under pressure; management will **lean into UA only when offer supply is strong enough to retain savers**. Retention is a function of **ongoing paid inventory**, not a leftover habit. — [IBTA Q2 2026 transcript excerpt](https://exa.ai/library/markets/stocks/IBTA/earnings/FY2026/Q2/transcript)
- Mobile retention generally: steep D1–D7 drop, D30 is the leaky-bucket metric; “great acquisition + bad retention is a doom loop.” No Ibotta/Rakuten public D30 was found. — [MWM retention glossary](https://mwm.ai/glossary/retention)
- Cashback apps that survive tend to be **attached to a real product** (bank cards) or to have scaled retailer supply (Rakuten/Ibotta/Fetch). Standalone “we pay you” apps die when they cut rates. — [Visu Network, cashback apps](https://visu.network/blog/cashback-apps-dying/)

### Inferences
- **Quest/airdrop analogue is the closest to “come for the money, stay for the crypto product.”** Measured D30 is ~1% or worse. Loofta should assume nano creators acquired via $20 campaigns behave like quest farmers, not like Venmo’s social graph.
- **GPT analogue is what Loofta would actually build if it leaned into quantum creators:** a rewards job board. Those users retain *if Loofta keeps listing paid gigs*. That is not a consumer send/claim/off-ramp business; it is Swagbucks with USDC.
- **Cashback analogue (Ibotta):** even a public company cannot retain D2C users without a firehose of paid offers. Transferring those users to “now send money to your friend” is a fantasy.
- The rare “come for money, stay for product” successes are when money is a **bonus on a product the user already needed** (bank cashback, Robinhood’s $5 for a brokerage account they use to trade). Loofta’s send/claim is not a daily habit for nano clippers in PH/NG unless they already have P2P demand.
- Whop/Relish clippers will churn from *Loofta* the moment the campaign budget ends, unless Loofta *is* the marketplace. Acquire-then-retain requires owning the next gig, not the wallet.

### Gaps
- No public D30 for Relish creators, Whop clippers, or Tremendous recipients returning as *payout-platform* users.
- No Layer3- or Galxe-disclosed cohort D30 that is independent of vendor case studies.
- Swagbucks/Prodege does not publish retention; GPT 15–30% D30 is an operator-blog benchmark, not audited.
- No study of converting GPT users into P2P payment-app users was found.

## Automation: do brands still need to pay humans by social handle, or do they pay bots/platforms?

### Takeaway
**AI is eating commodity UGC production (hooks, variants, testing), not authentic distribution.** Brands still pay humans to put content on *real accounts* (clipping, local Relish posts) because algorithms and FTC rules still price **real identity**. They will pay **platforms** (Whop, Insense, Relish) rather than random @handles, because bots and fake engagement are now a **civil-penalty** problem. Paying humans $5 to like/follow at scale is legally radioactive if the engagement is fake; paying real people to post with disclosure is still demanded.

### Cited Findings
- Agency/operator 2026 view: AI UGC **70–80% cheaper** per asset; wins high-volume paid-social testing above ~$50k/month ad spend. **Real creators win organic, trust, testimonials.** Below ~$15k/month paid social, human programs still win per dollar. Hybrid is the default in the $15k–$50k band. — [Social Operator, AI vs real UGC](https://socialoperator.ai/learn/ai-ugc-vs-real-ugc/)
- Spark UGC 2026: AI can match CTR (~1.5–3% on Meta) but **trust gap widened**—Fractl 2026 survey: share who say heavy AI use would make them trust a favorite brand less **20% → 40%**. Human UGC better on 30–60s conversion. NY **synthetic performer disclosure** in force **9 Jun 2026** for ads shown to NY audiences. — [Spark UGC, 2026](https://www.sparkugc.com/resources/ai-ugc-vs-real-creators-2026)
- Human UGC still commonly **$150–$500 per video** in 2026 agency copy vs “a few dollars in tool credits” for AI. — [The Remarkable](https://theremarkableagency.com/blog/ai-ugc-vs-real-creators-paid-ads/)
- Epidemic Sound survey of 3,000 UK/US professional creators (Mar–Apr 2026): **94% already use AI**; **75% see human-created content becoming a premium** in the AI era; 30% are creating fully AI-generated content. Acceleration, not replacement, is the authors’ frame. — [Epidemic Sound 2026 report](https://downloads.ctfassets.net/js6ap5wzepad/2EwMABVPOT9ToweqNnqzDP/ea0efbb8fe11a322f53163e9cf9cb2ca/The_Future_of_the_Creator_Economy_Report_2026.pdf)
- Later 2025: **92% of brands** already using or open to AI in influencer workflows—so the *buyer* is automating sourcing/ops, not necessarily replacing the human face. — [Later / PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/influencer-marketing-in-2025-new-data-reveals-what-works-what-costs-and-whats-next-302490369.html)
- **FTC Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule**, effective **21 Oct 2024** (16 C.F.R. Part 465): bans fake/false reviews and testimonials; bans incentives **conditioned on sentiment**; bans selling/buying **fake indicators of social media influence** (followers, views, likes, comments, shares, etc.) that misrepresent influence for a commercial purpose. Knowledge standard: knew or should have known. FTC staff warning letters to 10 companies in **Dec 2025**; penalties up to **$53,088** per knowing violation cited in 2026 industry writeups. Avatars/virtual influencers are **not categorically banned**, but fake experience testimonials are. Hiring real influencers to get **real** follows is explicitly **not** fake indicators. — [FTC Q&A](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers); [FTC 22 Aug 2024 final rule](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2024-08-22/pdf/2024-18519.pdf); [FTC 2025 warning letters](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/12/ftc-warns-10-companies-about-possible-violations-agencys-new-consumer-review-rule); [FTC influencer disclosures](https://www.bulkorder.ftc.gov/sites/bulkorder.ftc.gov/files/publications/1001a_influencer_guide_508_8_10_23.pdf)
- Relish campaign copy requires **FTC partnership disclosure** in the caption. Whop uses **AI review + view verification** because un-verified clip views are the fraud surface. — Relish campaign pages; [Whop Content Rewards](https://whop.com/blog/whop-content-rewards/)

### Inferences
- **Production** of talking-head ads is being automated. **Distribution via real social graphs** is not, because the algorithm and the FTC still care whether a person exists. That preserves clipping and local seeding.
- Brands will not want Loofta to be the place they “spray $5 to 10,000 handles.” They want a **marketplace with fraud controls** (Whop, Relish) and then a rail. If Loofta only does the rail, AI/automation is irrelevant except as it increases *volume of tiny payouts* those marketplaces send.
- Paying humans to manufacture engagement (likes/comments) without disclosure, or via bots, is the thing the 2024 rule was written to kill. A “quantum creator” product that looks like paid engagement farming is a regulatory trap; a product that looks like disclosed UGC/clipping is not.
- AI agents as *payees* (brands paying bot farms or synthetic clippers) collide with §465.8 if the indicators are fake. Expect more spend to **verified-human platforms**, which is good for Relish/Whop and only good for Loofta if those platforms need a better rail.

### Gaps
- No 2026 brand-spend split of AI-generated UGC vs human UGC (agency blogs assert direction, not dollars).
- No FTC enforcement action found that names a clipping marketplace.
- Fractl “20% → 40% trust” is cited by Spark UGC; original Fractl table was not fetched.

## Counter: Deel / Tipalti / Toku vs Tremendous — is “creator payroll” a category error?

### Takeaway
**Yes, if the target is professional creators. No, if the target is $5–$75 nano incentives—and then you must not call it creator payroll.** Deel/Toku/Gusto are employment/contractor payroll (onboarding, tax, recurring salary). Tipalti/Trolley/Gigapay are mass payables for platforms that already have creators as vendors. Tremendous (and Dots, in the gig-rail camp) is the correct analogue for quantum-creator checks. Competing with Deel for “real creator payroll” is a category error for a claim-by-email consumer app.

### Cited Findings
- **Tipalti** positions itself as the creator-payout API: portal/iFrame/API, KYC, W-8/W-9, 1099/1042-S, OFAC, **200+ countries, 120 currencies**, from **$249/month**. Named peers in its 2026 roundup: Stripe Connect, Trolley (from **$2,399/year**), Airwallex, **Gigapay (€279/mo + 4.9%)**, Payouts.com, PayPal Payouts ($0.25/txn). The job to be done is **compliant vendor pay**, not consumer wallets. — [Tipalti, 7 Oct 2026](https://tipalti.com/resources/learn/content-creator-payouts-api/)
- **Toku vs Deel:** Deel pays employees in USDC/EURC/USDT and contractors in proprietary **DLUSD**, but only if you run payroll *on Deel*. Toku adds stablecoin payroll beside ADP/Gusto. This is **salary**, not campaign seeding. — [Toku comparison](https://www.toku.com/stablecoin-payroll-comparison)
- **Gigapay:** brand uploads name/email/amount/country; Gigapay does KYC, self-billing, local rails (SEPA Instant, Faster Payments, ACH); one invoice back to finance. Creators claim. This *looks* like Loofta’s claim UX, but the buyer is **finance/compliance** and the ACV is software + 4.9% on **invoice-sized** payouts. — [Gigapay global payouts](https://www.gigapay.com/product/global-payouts); [Gigapay how it works](https://www.gigapay.com/why-gigapay/how-it-works)
- **Tremendous:** research/survey/marketing incentives; **$0 platform fee**; gift cards at face; Venmo/PayPal/Cash App/bank **4% sender or 6% recipient**; W-9; 1099. IRS threshold for research incentives **$2,000 in 2026** (up from $600 in 2025) per Tremendous’s Sep 2026 LinkedIn note. Forsta case (from adjacent research): **$2M to 175,000 participants** in 100+ countries (~$11 average). — [Tremendous research incentives](https://www.tremendous.com/solutions/research-incentives/); [Tremendous LinkedIn](https://linkedin.com/company/tremendous-rewards)
- **Dots vs Tremendous (Dots’ 2026 comparison, vendor-biased):** Tremendous = one-time rewards/gift cards; Dots = recurring gig/marketplace KYC, TIN, 1099, 300+ rails. Dots argues Tremendous is **not a paycheck**. That split is the same (b) vs (a) split in this memo. — [Dots vs Tremendous, Jul 2026](https://usedots.com/blog/dots-vs-tremendous-payout-api/)
- Relish $50–$75 and clipping $1 CPM land on the **Tremendous side of that split**. CreatorIQ median **$3,000 per campaign** lands on the **Gigapay/Tipalti** side.

### Inferences
- If Loofta pitches “creator payroll,” a mid-market agency’s finance team will ask for **one invoice, W-9, VAT, OFAC**—and buy Gigapay or Tipalti. Loofta’s consumer claim/off-ramp does not win that RFP.
- If Loofta pitches “pay 8,000 nano accounts $25 in NG/PH/ID without PayPal,” that is **Tremendous with USDC and social-handle identity**, and it is a coherent product. The buyers are Relish-like marketplaces, research ops, clipping platforms, and Web3 campaigns—not Nike’s influencer lead.
- Inventing a “quantum creator” class does not create a new budget. The budget already exists as **seeding, UGC production, clipping CPMs, and research incentives**. Relish already employs that class. The missing piece is not a new human type; it is **rails + claim** in geos where PayPal/Tremendous cash is expensive.
- Combining (a) and (b) in one TAM slide is how you get to a fake $32B SAM. Keep them on separate slides.

### Gaps
- Deel’s creator-specific volume (vs generic contractors) is not public.
- Tremendous does not publish what share of GMV is “influencer seeding” vs surveys.
- No contract-level comparison of Gigapay 4.9% vs Stripe Connect vs Loofta’s likely take-rate.

## Does the “quantum creator” acquire-then-retain loop work?

### Takeaway
**As stated, no.** You can acquire lots of mercenaries with $5–$75 gigs (Relish, Whop, Swagbucks, Galxe all prove acquisition). You cannot retain them on a consumer send/claim/off-ramp **because they came for the next payout, not for a wallet.** The loop only works if Loofta **is the ongoing gig marketplace** (become Whop/Relish/Swagbucks) or if the payout is a bonus on a payment product the user already needs (Ibotta-on-a-bank, not Ibotta-as-the-product). “Invent a new class of tiny creators” is marketing language for a labor pool that already exists and already churns.

### Cited Findings
- Supply already exists: UGCagent **26,791** UGC creators, median **~1,700** followers; Mordor says agencies already run CRM for “hundreds of nano relationships”; Relish operates in **60 cities** paying $50–$75. — [UGCagent](https://ugcagent.app/ugc-rates/nano-influencer-rates/); [Mordor](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/influencer-marketing-market); [Relish about](https://tryrelish.com/about)
- Income concentration is worsening, not democratizing: CreatorIQ top 10% **62%** of payments (2025). IMH: majority still **under $15k/year**. TikTok Rewards **excludes** <10k followers. There is no 2026 structural shift that turns nanos into retained professional users of a payout app. — [CreatorIQ](https://www.creatoriq.com/press/releases/state-of-creator-compensation-); [IMH](https://influencermarketinghub.com/creator-earnings-report-2025/); [TikTok terms](https://www.tiktok.com/legal/page/global/tiktok-creator-rewards-program-eea/en)
- Retention of money-first users: quest D30 **~1%**; Quest Love **~0.6% even after tokens**; GPT D30 15–30% **on the GPT site**; Ibotta D2C shrinking without offer supply. — sources in retention section
- Human UGC/clipping demand persists, but it accrues to **marketplaces with verification**, not to a generic handle-pay wallet. — Whop, CLIPPING, Relish, FTC rule

### Inferences
- **Loop, steelmanned:** Brand pays 2,000 nanos $25 via Loofta → they claim without a wallet → some keep the balance → some send to friends / off-ramp → Loofta gets a consumer graph. This is the same story every airdrop told. Measured conversion to unpaid usage is ~1%. Even 5% of 2,000 is 100 users who still have no reason to *send* unless their social graph is also on Loofta.
- **Loop that could work (different company):** Loofta becomes the **place nanos find the next Relish/Whop gig** (supply side) *and* the rail. Retention is “next $25,” same as Swagbucks. That can be a business (GPT sites make money) but it is not a consumer payments company and it fights Whop/Relish/clipping boards on their core loop.
- **Loop that matches Loofta’s actual product:** ignore “creators.” Sell **claim-by-email/handle + USDC off-ramp** to the companies that already pay many small humans (Tremendous customers in EM, clipping platforms, Meta-like platforms that do not want to own wallets, research panels). Acquisition of *recipients* is a byproduct, not the strategy. Do not forecast D30 consumer retention from those recipients.
- Recommended positioning if the company insists on the word “creator”: **“payouts for campaign supply (clippers, UGC, local seeders) in markets PayPal cannot pay,”** never **“we are building the creator economy’s payroll layer”** and never **“we will invent quantum creators and keep them.”**

### Gaps
- No longitudinal study of Relish/Whop payees adopting a third-party wallet/app after first payout.
- Loofta’s own giveaway-recipient D30 is not in this research set; it should be measured internally before betting on this loop.
- No 2026 figure for how many nano payees are unbanked vs just PayPal-frictioned (the latter is a rail win; the former is an off-ramp win).
