# AI agents, bots, and synthetic media vs handle payouts, giveaways, creator economies, and crypto onboarding (2025–2027)

Scope note. Three phenomena are mixed in popular “the internet is bots” talk and must be kept separate: (a) social engagement bots and purchased fake indicators; (b) AI-generated content replacing or supplementing human UGC; (c) autonomous agent commerce (wallets paying APIs). They have different economics, different legal treatments, and different product implications for a “pay @handles” rail. No source found in this pass supports the literal claim that “most of the internet is just bots” as a social-account census. What is well measured is cheaper fake engagement, high sybil rates in *incentivized* crypto quests, and brands still paying humans for authenticity while using AI for production.

## Measured bot share on X / TikTok / Instagram / Telegram (2024–2026) and crypto raid/quest bot economics

### Takeaway
Platform-wide “bot % of all accounts” is not a single number and is not “most.” The best-supported X figures cluster around ~20% of *active/visible* accounts or event discussants being bot-like/spam, not 50–90%. Instagram and TikTok enforcement against purchased fake engagement is weak (NATO StratCom 2025 experiment). Crypto quest platforms themselves now admit that paying per click/volume produces bots and wash; one growth vendor claimed 95–98% sybils on a partner’s historic quest traffic.

### Cited Findings

**X / Twitter — accounts vs activity vs company claims**
- A March 2025 *Scientific Reports* analysis of ~5 billion tweets from ~200 million users across 7 events, labeled with BotHunter, found bot users averaged **about 20%** across events and spiked to **43%** in US-election discussion. The paper treats 20% as a baseline for event chatter, consistent with Elon Musk’s 20% claim, and notes general samples usually land below 30%. — [Ng & Carley, Scientific Reports, 31 Mar 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-96372-1)
- SparkToro + Followerwonk (13–15 May 2022) sampled 44,058 public accounts that tweeted in the prior 90 days from 130M+ public active profiles and estimated **19.42%** were fake or spam under a conservative multi-signal score (margin ±0.486%). This is *recently tweeting public accounts*, not all accounts and not Twitter’s mDAU definition. — [SparkToro, 16 May 2022](https://sparktoro.com/blog/sparktoro-followerwonk-joint-twitter-analysis-19-42-of-active-accounts-are-fake-or-spam/)
- Twitter’s own SEC-era claim was **under 5%** of monetizable daily active users (mDAU) were spam/false accounts (Q1 2022 vintage; still the last official company census widely cited). Independent researchers summarized a **9–15% of accounts** range historically (Varol et al. 2017 “up to 15% of active accounts”). Account-share and tweet-volume-share are different: older Sysomos-era summaries put bots at ~24% of *tweets*. — [Twitter/X bot-statistics compilation citing Varol 2017 and company mDAU](https://www.twitterapis.com/twitter-statistics)
- BotPercent (arXiv 2302.00381, 2023) found bot interference is **not homogeneous**: politics and cryptocurrency communities show elevated bot rates; 8–14% of interactions with Musk’s Trump-reinstatement poll were bot. — [Yang et al., BotPercent, arXiv 2302.00381](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2302.00381)

**TikTok / Instagram / Facebook / YouTube — purchased fake engagement, not platform census**
- NATO Strategic Communications Centre of Excellence experiment, Sep–Nov 2025, published Jan 2026: researchers bought fake engagement from 10 commercial providers across Facebook, Instagram, YouTube, TikTok, VKontakte, X, and Bluesky. **>30,000 inauthentic accounts** delivered **>100,000 units** of fake engagement for **~€252**. Four weeks later most platforms left most fake accounts and engagement in place. — [PPC Land summary of NATO StratCom experiment, 1 Feb 2026](https://ppc.land/cheap-bots-overwhelm-platforms-despite-years-of-detection-promises/)
- Fake-account *removal* after 4 weeks: VK 96%, X 82%, YouTube/Bluesky 55%, Facebook 39%, Instagram **22%**, TikTok **4%**. Fake-*engagement* removal: X 57%, YouTube 44%, VK 30%, Facebook 21%, TikTok **17%**, Instagram **16%**, Bluesky 0%. — same NATO experiment, [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/cheap-bots-overwhelm-platforms-despite-years-of-detection-promises/)
- Standardized basket price (100 likes, 100 comments, 1,000 views, 100 followers): Bluesky ~€1.41, Instagram ~€1.73, Facebook ~€2.73, TikTok ~€2.36, X ~€12.08 (but X *views* were extremely cheap: 156,083 views for €10). Ready-to-use *advertising* accounts cost far more than standard fake accounts (e.g. TikTok ads accounts €3.59 vs €0.072 standard). — [PPC Land / NATO StratCom](https://ppc.land/cheap-bots-overwhelm-platforms-despite-years-of-detection-promises/)
- AI-enabled content factories: for €10, services generated 40–2,500 pieces of AI text/image/video; Cyabra analysis for the same experiment found **22%** of profiles in a Sep 2025 Russia-drone-in-Poland discourse sample were inauthentic, using AI imagery and local-looking personas. — [PPC Land / NATO StratCom](https://ppc.land/cheap-bots-overwhelm-platforms-despite-years-of-detection-promises/)
- Ad-fraud adjacent (web traffic, not social accounts): PPC Land cites early-2025 investigations that ~**40% of web traffic** was fake users/computerized bots, and DoubleVerify reporting **+101% YoY** bot fraud in 2024 with 16% linked to AI tools. This is advertising-inventory fraud, not a social-platform user census. — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/cheap-bots-overwhelm-platforms-despite-years-of-detection-promises/)
- Cambridge COTSI (Science paper based on 25 Jul 2024–27 Jul 2025 vendor data; news release 11 Dec 2025): SMS verification for a fake account averaged **$0.10 on X and Instagram**, **$0.11 on TikTok**, **$0.08 on Meta**, vs **$0.89 Telegram** and **$1.02 WhatsApp**. US/UK/Russia were among the cheapest geographies ($0.26 / $0.10 / $0.08); Japan $4.93, Australia $3.24. Telegram/WhatsApp fake-account prices rose **+12% / +15%** in the 30 days before 61 national elections. A 2022 Dek co-authored study cited in the release: ~€10 bought ~90,000 fake views or ~200 fake comments. — [EurekAlert / University of Cambridge, 11 Dec 2025](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1108524)

**Telegram — official bots vs fake members (do not conflate)**
- Politecnico di Torino / WWW Companion 2025 (“Topic-wise Exploration of the Telegram Group-verse,” arXiv 2409.02525): **official API bots** were present in **89.8%** of studied groups (median 4 bots/group) and generated **8.6% of messages** on average. That is *declared bots* (moderation, quizzes), not fake-human sockpuppets. Politics groups had the *lowest* bot-message share. The same paper notes heavy promotional spam in cryptocurrency groups. — [La Morgia et al. PDF via Politecnico di Torino](https://iris.polito.it/retrieve/f09f4aa2-5235-406c-b62f-5de7f3da6541/3701716.3717506.pdf)
- TGuard (vendor, 2025/26 channel-audit product): median across channels >10k subscribers is **~7% known-bot accounts** and **~15% dead/inactive**; 25%+ bots is a red flag, 30%+ implies attack or bought members. One anecdote: an 80k-subscriber channel at 8% ERR was **31% bots**. — [TGuard, Telegram Channel Analysis](https://tguard.pro/en/blog/telegram-channel-analysis/)
- Unsourced industry blogs repeat “30–50% of large Telegram groups / crypto channels are fake members” at **$1–5 per 1,000 members**. No primary dataset was attached to that range in this pass. Treat as market-hearsay, not a census. — [FakeFollowerCheck blog](https://fakefollowercheck.com/blog/fake-follower-statistics-2026/)

**Instagram / TikTok influencer-audience fraud (vendor studies; weaker)**
- SociaVault Labs (2026, 100k accounts, 50k IG + 50k TikTok, proprietary 12-indicator method): **37.2%** of influencer accounts showed “meaningful signs” of inauthentic followers/engagement; Instagram 41.8% vs TikTok 32.6%; macro tier (100k–500k) 48.3%. Method is not peer-reviewed; they themselves note high-quality NLP bot comments may evade the system. — [SociaVault Labs, Fake Follower Study 2026](https://sociavault.com/labs/reports/fake-follower-study-2026)

**Legal overlay (US) — buying fake engagement is not just a quality problem**
- FTC Endorsement Guides, 16 CFR Part 255 (2023 update), Example 13: purchasing or creating fake social-media followers to misrepresent influence for a commercial purpose is a **deceptive practice**; selling/distributing such indicators is also deceptive. The Guides also treat **virtual influencers** as endorsers. — [16 CFR Part 255](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-255)
- Binding rule (not just a guide): 16 CFR **Part 465** (Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule, effective 2024). §465.8 prohibits selling/distributing or purchasing/procuring **fake indicators of social media influence** (followers, views, likes, comments, etc. generated by bots, fake accounts, stolen identities, or hijacked accounts) when the actor knew or should have known they were fake and they materially misrepresent influence for a commercial purpose. — [16 CFR 465.8](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/465.8); [eCFR Part 465](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-465); [FTC Q&A](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers)
- FTC Q&A: hiring real influencers whose followers then follow you is **not** procuring fake indicators; hiring influencers who *happen* to have some fake followers is also not automatically a 465.8 violation. The rule is about **knowingly buying bot metrics**, not about every noisy audience. — [FTC Q&A](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers)

**Crypto quests / raids / airdrop farming**
- Snag Solutions (quest/growth vendor, 28 Jul 2025): “Today’s top questing platforms are bot farms.” Named Galxe, Zealy, and Layer3 as partners that “reward users (or bots) regardless of their contribution’s value.” Social tasks often go **unverified** because projects cannot afford X API. One partner’s historic campaign users: **>95% bots**; after Snag’s anti-sybil model, sybil share fell from **98% to 10%**, leaving a “core community of ~150k.” This is a vendor case study of a client that already knew it had a bot problem — not a census of all Galxe/Zealy traffic. — [Snag, “Why Teams Need to Optimize for More Than Just Metrics,” 28 Jul 2025](https://www.snagsolutions.io/blog/bot-farms)
- Galxe Help Center (updated 10 Aug 2026): documents a Bot Identification System (behavioral + on-chain). **Reward disqualification is not default** — it is active for GG Rewards Hub, Gigapot, and quests that enable it on **Business+ Growth/Enterprise**. Spaces that do not add sybil-prevention credentials allow “participation from multiple or new accounts without restriction — this is expected behavior, not a bug.” Premium “Advanced Sybil Prevention” is a paid upsell. — [Galxe Bot Identification System](https://help.galxe.com/en/articles/11827806-galxe-bot-identification-system)
- Zealy (Mathis Grosjean, 14 Aug 2026; anti-cheat claims rechecked 17 Aug 2026 against Zealy source): **“Zealy does not verify that on-chain activity is authentic.”** Token/NFT tasks read a balance; the task named “onChain” is a webhook to the project’s own HTTPS endpoint. Anti-cheat links reused email / social / wallet identifiers; it does **not** cluster separate wallets operated by one person. Zealy’s “Proof of Humanity” task is an X-account heuristic (avatar, follower floors, a model) — “it reads no chain data.” Zealy’s core argument: **pay-per-transaction produces wash**. Evidence cited: hildobby/Dune Dec 2022 — 58% of 2022 Ethereum NFT volume had wash signatures, **LooksRare 98%, X2Y2 87%, OpenSea 2.4%** (LooksRare/X2Y2 paid trade-to-earn tokens; OpenSea did not). Chainalysis Jan 2025: up to **$2.57B** suspected wash trading on ETH/BSC/Base in 2024, but only **0.035–0.046%** of November 2024 DEX *volume*. LayerZero (May 2024): ~6M unique interacting wallets; **803,093** addresses identified as potential sybils (from an initial pool >2M). Arbitrum (DL News, Jun 2023): one funding wallet → 1,000+ accounts → >428,000 ARB. — [Zealy, “How to Drive On-Chain Activity, Not Wash Volume,” 14 Aug 2026](https://zealy.io/blog/how-to-drive-on-chain-activity)
- Independent 2026 explainer (The Signal): any financially valuable quest attracts farming; recommended metrics are unique *verified* users after sybil filtering, core product actions, cohort retention, and **cost per retained user** — not completions. — [The Signal, quest platforms 2026](https://thesignal.directory/n/quest-platforms-community-incentives-2026)

### Inferences
- “Most of the internet is bots” is false as a *user census* on X/TikTok/Instagram in 2025–2026. ~15–20% of active X discussants being bot-like, plus cheap purchased engagement that platforms do not remove, is the grounded picture. Telegram *declared bots* are ubiquitous in groups but are mostly tools, not fake humans; *purchased members* in crypto channels are a real commercial product with weak public measurement.
- Where Loofta’s giveaway/raid/quest surface pays **per like, reply, raid, follow, or on-chain click**, the 2022–2026 record says the buyer will receive manufactured volume. Snag’s 95–98% figure is one tainted funnel, not every campaign, but Galxe/Zealy’s own docs concede that anti-sybil is optional, paid, or identifier-reuse-only.
- Engagement *volume* is not scarce. **Cheap SIMs + cheap likes** (Cambridge $0.10/account; NATO ~€2 per 100-like basket on IG/TikTok) mean a handle is an address, not a person. Scarcity starts only after uniqueness + liveness + “this action cannot be farmed.”

### Gaps
- No Similarweb or SensorTower platform-level bot-share series was found in open results (likely paywalled). Cannot trend “bot share 2024 vs 2026” from those vendors.
- No peer-reviewed platform census of TikTok or Instagram *accounts* comparable to SparkToro/Scientific Reports on X.
- No public Galxe/Zealy/Layer3 *platform-wide* sybil rate; only vendor case studies, help-center product limits, and airdrop post-mortems (LayerZero, Arbitrum).
- Telegram 30–50% fake-member claim lacks a named dataset.
- “Engagement pods” (human collusion rings) are widely described in influencer-fraud taxonomies (SociaVault lists pods as 20% of fraud types in their sample) but this pass did not find a 2024–2026 measured pod-share for X/TikTok.

## AI UGC: brands generating ads without humans vs still buying human authenticity; nano-creator rate pressure

### Takeaway
Brands are adopting AI for **editing, briefs, variants, and some synthetic ads**, not as a full substitute for human creators. US creator ad spend is still growing fast (IAB: $37B in 2025). Advertiser concern about losing “human connection” is near-universal (95%). Virtual-influencer *willingness* among brands **fell** from 86% to 60% on Collabstr between Oct 2024 and Jul 2025. Human nano/UGC rates in 2025 were still in the $40–$200/deliverable band; AI production cost is dollars-to-tens-of-dollars per variant. That is price pressure on **content-only UGC used as ad creative**, not yet a collapse of paying humans who have a real audience and a face.

### Cited Findings
- IAB + Advertiser Perceptions (Nov 2025), survey of **>450** US ad-spend decision-makers: US creator-economy **intentional ad spend** $13.9B (2021) → $29.5B (2024) → **$37B projected 2025** (+26% YoY, ~4× overall media +5.7%). Creators are a “must buy” for 48% of spenders (third after social and paid search). Top creator-selection criteria: reputation 58%, audience alignment 56%. Identifying the right creators is the top operational challenge (~1/3). Brands want “standards for audience authentication and fraud prevention.” — [IAB PR, 20 Nov 2025](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/creator-economy-ad-spend-to-reach-37-billion-in-2025-growing-4x-faster-than-total-media-industry-according-to-iab-302621013.html); [IAB report PDF](https://www.iab.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/IAB_Creator_Ad_Spend_and_Strategy_Report_2025.pdf)
- Same IAB study: **three in four** brands using/planning AI for creator-marketing tasks. Of those using AI: content editing 49%, creator briefs 46%, content personalization 45%; **one-third using it to make synthetic content**. Uses are “centered around efficiency, scalability, and content refinement” rather than full creative replacement. **95% of advertisers have concerns** about AI in creator marketing; top concern is **loss of human connection**, which IAB says is the top reason they invest in creator marketing. — [IAB PR](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/creator-economy-ad-spend-to-reach-37-billion-in-2025-growing-4x-faster-than-total-media-industry-according-to-iab-302621013.html); [IAB PDF key insights](https://www.iab.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/IAB_Creator_Ad_Spend_and_Strategy_Report_2025.pdf)
- Goldman Sachs Global Investment Research, *Creator Economy Framing* (26 Mar 2025): AI disrupts via (a) UGC tools on creator platforms, (b) data/content licensing to AI labs, (c) **AI avatars** for monetization/engagement. eMarketer cited in coverage: US influencer marketing ~**$26B in 2023**, ~1/3 of US social digital ad spend. Coverage of the same report: ~67M creators in 2025 rising to **107M by 2030**. Creator-platform funding: $3.6B (2021) → $2.3B (2022) → $0.95B (2023) → **$1.5B (2024)**, rebound partly attributed to gen-AI tools. Goldman also flagged possible 2025 influencer-spend *pullback* under TikTok-US uncertainty (scenario, not a measured drop). — [Goldman PDF via CreatorsWithInfluence](https://creatorswithinfluence.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Goldman-Sachs-Global-Investment-Research-Creator-Economy-Framing-Market-Opportunity-Download-Report-March-26-2025.pdf); [NetInfluencer summary](https://www.netinfluencer.com/goldman-sachs-sees-rise-of-content-creators-to-107-million-by-2030/)
- Collabstr 2026 Influencer Marketing Report (2025 marketplace data of >21,000 collaborations), as tabulated in a secondary playbook checked Oct 2026: average **paid** fees — YouTube $255, Instagram $193, TikTok $186, **UGC (content delivered to brand) $154**, X $76; **~80% of collaborations under $300**. Collabstr told Digiday (11 Sep 2025) that the share of brands agreeing to include **AI creators** in campaigns fell from **86% (Oct 2024) to 60% (Jul 2025)**. WFA study of 27 multinational brands (Apr 2025, via Digiday): **60% had no plans** to adopt virtual influencers; 15% had tested; of those with no plans, **96% cited consumer trust**. — [IImagined playbook citing Collabstr/Digiday/WFA, updated 11 Oct 2026](https://iimagined.ai/blog/ai-influencer-brand-deals-how-to-land-sponsorships)
- Consumer attitude snapshots (via a 2026 industry comparison piece; prefer primaries where named): Ipsos Consumer Tracker Aug 2025 — ~2 in 3 Americans want **humans, not AI**, making marketing; Kantar US Media Reactions Sep 2025 — >60% worry gen-AI could lead to fake/misleading ads; Klaviyo 2026 AI Consumer Trends (7,998 consumers, 8 countries, Nov–Dec 2025) — 32% say low-quality/generic AI brand content reduces trust. These are **attitude** surveys, not conversion RCTs of AI-avatar ads vs human UGC. — [Digital Applied, AI Avatar Ads vs Real UGC, 2026](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/ai-avatar-ads-vs-real-ugc-creators-cost-trust-2026)
- Nano rate cards (agency blog, not a marketplace dump): 2024 nano posts ~$10–$100; 2025 averages cited **$40–$150/post**; TikTok nano video $50–$150 (up to $250 for complex); **content-only UGC $100–$200/video**; usage/whitelisting +30–50%. The same post *asserts* (without a time series) that “as AI content floods social media, human authenticity is becoming a luxury good” and nano face-on-camera rates should rise. That last sentence is opinion, not a measured 2024→2026 price index. — [Scrumball, Nano Influencer Rates 2025–2026](https://www.scrumball.com/blog/nano-influencer-rates)
- AI production cost: tool-vendor posts claim replacing $150–$1,000 human UGC videos with ~$6–$50/month of model+orchestration cost and “3.6x ROAS” case studies. These are marketing claims, not audited brand panels. They do establish that **marginal cost of extra ad variants is collapsing**. — [Stork.AI / Nano Banana Pro](https://www.stork.ai/blog/googles-ai-creates-infinite-ads)
- Weak/contradictory AI-influencer share claims: SnoopReport (2025 case-study blog) attributes to HypeAuditor 2025 that AI influencers account for **>15% of branded-content engagement on Instagram** (tripled since 2023) and that human brand collaborations fell 23% while AI sponsored content rose 240%. **This pass did not retrieve the HypeAuditor PDF itself.** Treat as unverified until the primary report is checked; it conflicts with Collabstr/WFA cooling and IAB’s “human connection” finding. — [SnoopReport](https://blog.snoopreport.com/ai-influencers-instagram-2025-case-study/)
- Disclosure law is tightening around synthetic spokespeople: FTC 2023 Endorsement Guides cover virtual influencers; Instagram began limiting reach of unlabeled AI-person profiles (playbook cites **31 Aug 2026**); NY synthetic-performer ad law in effect **Jun 2026**; EU AI Act Art. 50 transparency from **2 Aug 2026**. A Journal of Marketing 2024 study (cited in the same playbook) found consumers do not credit virtual influencers with touch/smell/taste, and such endorsements lowered purchase intention. — [IImagined playbook / FTC 16 CFR 255](https://iimagined.ai/blog/ai-influencer-brand-deals-how-to-land-sponsorships)

### Inferences
- Split the market. **Ad-creative UGC** (a talking-head video the brand runs as a paid ad, no need for the creator’s followers) is the slice under direct AI price pressure. **Distribution UGC** (a real person posting to a real audience that chose to follow them) is what brands told IAB they are buying: reputation, audience alignment, human connection. Nano rates in 2025 were *up* vs 2024 in at least one rate-card compilation, which is the opposite of a crash — consistent with authenticity premium, or with more demand, or with biased agency blogs. There is **no public 2024–2026 nano-CPM time series** proving a crash.
- Brands will still pay humans for: (1) first-person product use they can legally claim, (2) a trusted niche audience, (3) reputation/risk transfer, (4) comments and community that look like a real social graph, (5) talent that can show up on camera/live. They will pay AI for: variants, localization, always-on characters in fashion/beauty *when labeled*, and editing.
- “Quantum creators” (tiny humans paid to *engage* — likes, raid replies) sit on the wrong side of this split. Paid *content* from a verified tiny human is the authenticity product. Paid *engagement* from tiny humans is economically identical to a more expensive bot farm and legally adjacent to 16 CFR 465.8 if the engagement is then used as a commercial influence metric.

### Gaps
- No a16z “State of Crypto” or a16z consumer-AI memo with quantified AI-UGC vs human spend was retrieved in this pass (Goldman + IAB were the available majors).
- No independent nano-creator rate index (only marketplace averages at Collabstr, which mix tiers, and agency blogs).
- HypeAuditor 2025 primary PDF not fetched; SnoopReport’s 15%/240% figures should not be used in a final report without that PDF.
- No RCT of AI-avatar ads vs human UGC conversion at matched spend.

## Platform responses: X paid verification, TikTok authenticity labels, World ID, Privacy Pass, KYC-gated payouts

### Takeaway
Platforms did **not** solve bots with a blue check. X’s paid check is a subscription plus weak eligibility, and the EU fined X €120M for treating it as identity. TikTok’s material response is **AI-content labels + C2PA + originality rules for Creator Rewards**, plus tax/payment onboarding — not proof-of-personhood. The scarce new asset is **reusable uniqueness** (World ID / Humanity Protocol class) and **device-attested humanity** (Privacy Pass / Apple PAT), which prove “a human/device passed a check” without being a social handle. Payout rails (TikTok Rewards, PayPal/Hyperwallet) already require tax identity; that is KYC of the *payee*, not of the handle.

### Cited Findings
- X Premium: blue check = active **paid subscription**, not the legacy “notable and authentic” review. Requirements include display name, profile photo, confirmed phone, activity in last 30 days, not misleading/spam. Premium also added optional **government-ID verification** with an “ID verified” label, distinct from the paid check. X says the changes are “designed to reduce fake, untrustworthy accounts.” Verified replies are prioritized. — [X Premium help, archive](https://archive.ph/AqTHt)
- European Commission fine: **€120 million** against X over “deceptive” blue ticks — the Commission said paying for a check “deceives users” because X is not “meaningfully verifying” who is behind the account; also advertising transparency and researcher-access issues. — [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c4g9kejzvw0o)
- X product response beyond the check: Head of Product Nikita Bier outlined profile transparency (account age, detected location, username history, usage patterns) to help users spot bots; a secondary recap cites a recent purge of **1.7 million** reply-spam bots. These are signals, not uniqueness proofs. — [Storyy recap of X profile-data experiment](https://storyy.com/insights/x-to-display-more-profile-data-to-combat-bots-and-build-trust)
- TikTok AIGC: creators must label realistic AI-generated/significantly AI-edited images, audio, and video. TikTok auto-labels some AIGC, including uploads carrying **C2PA Content Credentials**; TikTok said it was the first video-sharing platform to implement C2PA credentials and that **>37 million creators** had used its AI-label tool. — [TikTok Support, AI-generated content](https://support.tiktok.com/en/using-tiktok/creating-videos/ai-generated-content); [TikTok Newsroom, C2PA](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/partnering-with-our-industry-to-advance-ai-transparency-and-literacy)
- TikTok Creator Rewards (US terms): original 1-minute+ non-promotional video, 1,000 qualified FYP views; account eligibility includes follower/view thresholds in Academy copy; **tax onboarding required**; payouts via PayPal/Hyperwallet; **$50 minimum**; TikTok “does not process” the payment. Labeled AI content is not automatically barred, but originality/quality rules and Code of Conduct apply. This is **KYC/tax of the creator as payee**, not a proof that every viewer is human. — [TikTok Creator Rewards US terms](https://www.tiktok.com/legal/page/global/creator-rewards-program-us/en); [EEA terms](https://www.tiktok.com/legal/page/global/tiktok-creator-rewards-program-eea/en); [Creator Academy](https://www.tiktok.com/creator-academy/en/article/creator-rewards-program)
- World ID: privacy-preserving **proof of unique human** (Orb iris and, where available, NFC passport/government ID; selfie-check as a lower-assurance sybil_score). Designed so apps learn “unique human” without name/email/phone/social profile; nullifier prevents double-claim of a reward. Stated uses: gate signups, kill bots, one-person-one-vote, **fair reward distribution**. Integrations named: Razer, Match Group, Okta Auth0. Monthly WLD claims are a separate opt-in from verification. — [World ID](https://world.org/world-id); [World developer docs](https://docs.world.org/world-id/SKILL); [World ID FAQs](https://world.org/blog/world/world-id-faqs)
- World Network scale (company figures, not audited census): **10M verified humans** (Jan 2025, Biometric Update); **12M verified / 26M users** (May 2025 US launch post); **>14M verified / >30M World App users / >500M transactions** (24 Jul 2025 “Year two”); year-end 2025 post: **nearly 38M users**, Orb-verified count as a live ticker, World App claimed most-used self-custody wallet by MAU in Sep 2025, virtual accounts in 18 countries to receive paychecks in **USDC**. Legal blocks in parts of EU, Chile, HK, Dominican Republic, etc. — [Biometric Update, 13 Jan 2025](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202501/world-network-reaches-10-million-verified-humans-amid-continued-legal-blocks); [World, “At Last,” May 2025](https://world.org/blog/announcements/at-last-trust-in-the-age-of-ai); [World, Year two, 24 Jul 2025](https://dev.world.org/blog/announcements/world-year-two); [World in 2025](https://world.org/blog/announcements/world-in-2025)

Phone-verified ≠ unique human — it is a **$0.10 commodity** on X/Instagram. — [Cambridge / EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1108524)

### Inferences
- Paid verification failed as identity. It raised the cost of a *featured* bot somewhat and gave X a revenue line; the EU’s theory is that it *increased* impersonation risk by looking like ID. Phone verification failed as uniqueness (Cambridge prices).
- What actually got productized 2024–2026: (1) **content provenance** (C2PA labels) for synthetic media; (2) **payee KYC/tax** on creator payout programs; (3) **proof-of-personhood networks** (World, Humanity) aiming to be the reusable “not a sybil” credential; (4) **anonymous device tokens** (PAT/Privacy Pass) for bot walls, not for paying people.
- For a payout product, Privacy Pass is the wrong primitive (unlinkable, not a payee). World ID / passport NFC / government KYC are the right class if the goal is “one human, one claim.” A social handle is none of these.

- No TikTok-published share of FYP that is labeled AIGC.
- World’s verified-human counts are company announcements; regulators have ordered data deletion in some jurisdictions, so global “verified” is not a clean unique-human census.
- Similarweb/SensorTower authenticity or bot-traffic products not accessed.

## If payouts require KYC/liveness, does “pay by handle” collapse into “pay a verified person who happens to have a handle”?

### Takeaway
**Yes, for the money-movement and anti-sybil layer. No, for the UX and discovery layer.** The handle remains the *routing alias* people already type (“pay @alice”). The scarce, legally and economically necessary object becomes a **verified unique person (or KYCed payee) bound to that alias**. Unbound handle-payout is what bot farms arbitrage. Bound handle-payout is how you keep the consumer mental model while not paying 95% sybils.

### Cited Findings
- Cambridge COTSI: creating a plausible X/Instagram/TikTok account is priced in **cents** in the US/UK/RU. Telegram is dearer (~$0.89) because the phone number is visible and local. — [EurekAlert / Cambridge](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1108524)
- Galxe: sybil credentials are optional per quest; without them, multi-accounting is “expected.” Reward exclusion is a paid/enterprise feature. — [Galxe Help](https://help.galxe.com/en/articles/11827806-galxe-bot-identification-system)
- Zealy: connecting a wallet or an X account does not prove a person; Proof of Humanity is an X heuristic; anti-cheat catches identifier reuse, not parallel identities. “One wallet is not one person.” LayerZero 803k sybil wallets. — [Zealy, 14 Aug 2026](https://zealy.io/blog/how-to-drive-on-chain-activity)
- World ID developer docs: flagship `proofOfHuman` is for “Sybil resistance, airdrops, one-vote-per-human, gated signups”; apps must store the **nullifier** or the same human double-claims. Passport preset is for regulated/KYC-adjacent flows. World ID is explicitly **not** tied to social profiles. — [World developer docs](https://docs.world.org/world-id/SKILL); [World ID product](https://world.org/world-id)
- TikTok/PayPal payouts already require tax identity of the *creator*, while the public-facing identity remains the TikTok handle. That is the collapse in production today on a major platform. — [TikTok Creator Rewards US terms](https://www.tiktok.com/legal/page/global/creator-rewards-program-us/en)
- FTC 465.8 + 255 Example 13: commercial use of bot-generated followers/likes is deceptive. A product that pays handles to manufacture those indicators is in the blast radius; a product that pays a verified person for disclosed content is the influencer industry as IAB measures it. — [16 CFR 465.8](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/465.8); [16 CFR 255](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-255)
- IAB 2025: brands’ top selection criteria are **reputation and audience alignment**, and they are asking for “audience authentication and fraud prevention” as a standards gap — i.e. they already do not want to pay a handle, they want to pay a *credible person with a handle*. — [IAB PR](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/creator-economy-ad-spend-to-reach-37-billion-in-2025-growing-4x-faster-than-total-media-industry-according-to-iab-302621013.html)

### Inferences
- Product translation: `pay(@handle)` without binding = paying an SMS-verified sockpuppet for $0.10. `pay(@handle | unique_human_nullifier | tax_id)` = Venmo/PayPal-style alias on top of a person. The second is how every successful consumer payout product already works (email/phone as alias, KYC behind it at some threshold).
- Liveness-only (selfie, PAT, Turnstile) raises bot cost but does not cap **human farms** (“quantum creators” as many low-paid people). Uniqueness (World ID nullifier, government ID, one-payout-instrument) is what stops N accounts per operator. Engagement marketing needs both, plus a reward that cannot be manufactured (see Zealy: do not pay per countable action).
- Crypto onboarding that is “connect wallet + follow @project” is the 2023–2025 failure mode. Onboarding that is “unique human, then a wallet, then a handle as public name” is the 2026 World/Humanity thesis. Handle-first onboarding remains the right *UX* because that is how people address each other.

### Gaps
- No public A/B of conversion for “pay @handle” vs “pay after World ID / KYC” on a consumer crypto payout app.
- Unclear where US money-transmitter/KYC thresholds force identity behind every micro-payout vs batched/thresholded KYC (jurisdiction-specific; not researched here).

## Agents as economic actors: x402, Coinbase agent payments, Skyfire — real GMV or vapor?

### Takeaway
The **rails are real**; independent **seller GMV is tiny**. x402 on Base crossed **100M transactions** by ~Q1 2026, but Chainalysis says much of the spike was **PING meme pay-to-mint**, not agent commerce. An August 2026 scrape of Coinbase’s x402 Bazaar catalog estimated **~$11.7k then ~$10.2k** independent 30-day GMV. Skyfire has protocols, Visa/Experian demos, and funding — **no disclosed GMV**. Companies are not yet substituting “pay the agent’s wallet” for “pay the creator.” Agent payments are a **machine-to-API** market (search, data, inference), orthogonal to social-handle social proof.

### Cited Findings
- Coinbase x402: HTTP 402 Payment Required revival; default USDC on Base; announced **May 2025** in secondary summaries; contributed to Linux Foundation **x402 Foundation in April 2026**. Handshake for agent-payable APIs, not a full commerce stack. — [Nevermined comparison](https://nevermined.ai/blog/crossmint-vs-skyfire-vs-coinbase-x402); [Eco support explainer](https://eco.com/support/en/articles/15182332-stablecoin-liquidity-routing-for-ai-agents-multi-source-coordination-for-agentic-commerce)
- Chainalysis (3 Jun 2026): x402 agentic txs on Base from near-zero mid-2025 to **>100 million cumulative through Q1 2026**. Q4 2025 surge “driven in large part by meme coin activity, particularly PING”: pay 1 USDC to mint; **>10,000%** tx spike in a week; PING **>150,000 txs in first month**. Q1 2026 growth moderated. $1+ txs went from 49% of volume (early 2025) to **95%** (early 2026); 10¢–$1 share collapsed 46%→4%. Oct 2025 PING farmers pushed weekly retention to 87% then **5%**. Chainalysis: “mass adoption remains distant”; users are crypto-native. They still argue infrastructure is past pure PoC. — [Chainalysis, 3 Jun 2026](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/x402-agentic-payments-adoption/)
- Independent catalog measurement (dhughes6071 / driftwatch, scrape of Coinbase CDP x402 Bazaar on **7 Aug 2026** and re-run **23 Aug 2026**): 14,128 discoverable services; only 2.0% had ≥100 paid calls in 30 days; **estimated catalog GMV ~$11,748 / 30 days**; top independent operator ~$872–$1,100/month; Tavily ~$554 then **~$97/month**. ### Inferences
- Do not model 2026–2027 Loofta revenue on “brands will pay agent wallets instead of people.” The observed x402 economy is **micropayments for APIs and a meme mint**, with independent sellers earning three figures a month.
- The structural point that *does* transfer: when the buyer is software, the payee is a **wallet + policy** (Skyfire’s “which human authorized this agent”), not an @handle. If Loofta ever pays *agents* (e.g. a brand’s agent buying a giveaway slot, or a creator’s agent accepting USDC), the identity product is **Know Your Agent bound to a human principal** — the same binding as handle-to-person, one level up.
- Social proof remains a human-market problem. An agent cannot be an FTC-valid endorser of lived product experience (FTC: cannot talk about experience you have not had; virtual influencers already hit this). Agent commerce does not replace creator payouts; it might eventually sit beside them for tooling (agents buying data/inference that creators use).

## Implication for Loofta: verification + escrow vs engagement-for-pay; is paying @handles still a thing?

### Takeaway
Paying people **by social handle as the address** remains a thing and becomes *more* valuable as organic social fills with cheap bots and cheap synthetic content — **if and only if** the handle is bound to a scarce identity and the action paid for cannot be farmed. Paying people **for engagement** (raids, likes, quest clicks, “quantum” commenters) is the poisoned product: it is what bot farms sell, what FTC 465.8 targets, what Snag/Zealy/Galxe now warn against, and what NATO showed platforms will not clean up. The 2025–2027 product that fits the evidence is **identity-bound micro-payouts + escrow for real humans** (creators, winners, off-ramped USDC), not a raid/engagement marketplace.

### Cited Findings
- Handle as address is how consumers already think; payee KYC is how TikTok Rewards already pays. — [TikTok Creator Rewards terms](https://www.tiktok.com/legal/page/global/creator-rewards-program-us/en)
- IAB $37B 2025 creator ad spend, 95% AI-concern, demand for audience authentication. — [IAB PR, 20 Nov 2025](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/creator-economy-ad-spend-to-reach-37-billion-in-2025-growing-4x-faster-than-total-media-industry-according-to-iab-302621013.html)
- NATO: fake engagement still ~€2/100 likes on IG/TikTok and mostly not removed. — [PPC Land / NATO StratCom](https://ppc.land/cheap-bots-overwhelm-platforms-despite-years-of-detection-promises/)
- Cambridge: fake X/IG account ~$0.10. — [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1108524)
- Snag: quest platforms as bot farms; 95–98% sybil in one historic funnel. — [Snag, 28 Jul 2025](https://www.snagsolutions.io/blog/bot-farms)
- Zealy: paying per countable on-chain/social action produces wash; uniqueness ≠ wallet. — [Zealy, 14 Aug 2026](https://zealy.io/blog/how-to-drive-on-chain-activity)
- FTC 16 CFR 465.8: commercial fake indicators. — [16 CFR 465.8](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/465.8)
- World ID: built specifically to make scarce rewards one-per-human. World App 2025: USDC paycheck rails in 18 countries for verified humans. — [World ID](https://world.org/world-id); [World in 2025](https://world.org/blog/announcements/world-in-2025)
- x402 independent GMV ~$10k/month: agent pay is not the 2026 substitute for creator pay. — [driftwatch measurement, Aug 2026](https://github.com/dhughes6071/driftwatch/blob/main/research/posts/x402-economy-measured.md)

### Inferences
- **Is paying by social handle still a thing under automation?** Yes as UX: @handle/email is the public name. No as trust: the handle is a 10-cent commodity. The product is “pay this handle because we have bound it to a unique human and (when required) a tax identity,” which is already the shape of Venmo, PayPal, TikTok Rewards, and World ID claims.
- **Giveaways / raids / engagement marketing:** organic raid-as-growth is dying as a *measurable* channel because the metric is cheaper to fake than to earn (NATO prices; Snag funnels). Brands that still “do raids” are buying a vanity number unless they gate on uniqueness and pay for outcomes that are not likes (held liquidity, KYCed sign-up, disclosed UGC, unique-human raffle). Loofta’s crypto-giveaway surface is viable as **escrowed prize → unique claimant**; it is toxic as **pay-per-engagement quest**.
- **Creator economy:** human authenticity is the budgeted thing (IAB). AI eats undifferentiated talking-head ad creative. Identity-bound nano payouts get *more* valuable if organic reach is polluted — brands will overpay for a **proven person with a small real graph** relative to a large fake graph. That supports Loofta paying verified tiny humans for *content and prizes*, not for comments.
- **“Quantum creators”:** if this means many verified humans making disclosed short content / contest entries / local language UGC, it matches the IAB authenticity demand and the AI-flood premium. If it means many humans paid $0.05 to like/raid, it is a human CAPTCHA farm: more expensive than bots, still fake indicators under 465.8 if used commercially, and the same adverse selection as Galxe social tasks.
- **Crypto onboarding:** “follow, retweet, connect wallet” is how you onboard bots (Snag, LayerZero, Zealy). “Proof of person → wallet → USDC → optional handle alias” is how World is trying to onboard humans. Loofta already sits on the USDC/off-ramp side of that second path.
- **Verdict on the two Loofta theses:** (1) *Verification + escrow becomes the product* — **supported** by 2024–2026 evidence. (2) *Engagement-for-pay becomes poison* — **supported**. The opposite thesis (*identity-bound micro-payouts become more valuable because bots ate organic reach*) is also **supported**, and is not actually the opposite: it is the same thesis. What becomes more valuable is paying **verified humans** through handles; what becomes worthless is paying **handles**.

### Gaps
- No Loofta-internal conversion data in this research pass (product implication is analogical from adjacent markets).
- No measured 2026 brand budget split “raid/engagement” vs “escrowed contest/UGC” in crypto specifically.
- US/EU licensing constraints on paying unverified handles in USDC were not in scope.
